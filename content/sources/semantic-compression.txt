Abstract
Human beings and technical systems cannot operate without compression. Complex structures
are reduced into words, categories, statuses, metrics, scores, summaries, and models because
reality cannot be reconstructed from first principles every time a person or system must
communicate or decide. Compression is therefore not a defect. It is infrastructure for cognition
and coordination.
The failure begins when the compressed representation is granted more authority than the
condition it represents. A label can remain linguistically accurate while hiding distinctions that
change classification, permissible action, evidence requirements, cost exposure, rollback,
accountability, or claim strength. Different mechanisms can be collapsed into one term. The
same mechanism can be hidden behind different terms. A representation can remain stable
while the underlying condition changes. A statement can remain factually true while becoming
structurally misleading through omission.
Version 1.4 closes the propagation and certification-governance defects identified by adversarial
review of v1.3. It removes stale active-version attribution from the taxonomy and
fidelity/custody sections; performs a full-document recurrence sweep for version-relative
language; and places the Appendix C certification itself under an explicit Compression Contract
with observable revalidation triggers, enforcement states, and a violation response.
Reality Primacy does not reject abstraction. It denies abstraction
final authority over the condition it claims to represent.

Most of the paper is definitional, derived, or architectural. Where a statement is intended as a

1. The Problem Is Not the Word
A familiar word can create the feeling of understanding before understanding exists.
Intelligence, safety, execution, success, failure, employment, compliance, learning, governance,
harm, and proof can each carry several different mechanisms beneath one surface label. The
word allows communication to move quickly. The same speed can conceal unresolved structure.
When a listener receives a familiar label, the listener reconstructs some underlying state. If the
reconstruction is sufficient for the present operation, communication works. If it is not, the label
creates false confidence: the speaker believes information was supplied, the listener believes
meaning was received, and the structure required to distinguish the relevant states never
crossed the gap.
Semantic recognition is not structural understanding.
The useful question is therefore not only "What does this word mean?" but "What operative
structure has been compressed into this word, and which distinctions would change what may
rationally be classified, decided, executed, validated, or claimed?"
2. Compression Is Necessary
No person or system can carry the full causal, temporal, physical, social, and institutional
structure of every object into every operation. "Car" replaces thousands of components and
relationships. "Employee" replaces a labor arrangement. "Safe" replaces a risk state. "Done" may
replace a chain of requested, accepted, attempted, executed, observed, validated, and persisted
states.
Compression lowers cognitive load. It accelerates coordination. It allows expertise to be
packaged into reusable terms. It allows organizations to operate through dashboards instead of
raw logs and allows people with shared context to communicate through shorthand.
The objective is therefore not maximum detail. Maximum detail is often unusable. The objective
is sufficient resolution for the current operation.
Good compression preserves the distinctions required by the
decision being made.

3. Materiality, Bounded
Definition.
Materiality is operation-relative. It is not triggered by any imaginable difference and it is not
limited to differences already observed to have caused failure. A distinction is material only
inside a declared decision context.
For operation O, authority scope A, decision rules D, evidence rule E, and operating envelope
Omega, candidate states X and Y form a material distinction when treating X and Y as
equivalent would permit a different downstream transition in at least one admissible state
inside Omega while all unrelated conditions are held constant.
The declaration of O, A, D, E, and Omega is part of the test. Without them, materiality is
unresolved rather than presumed.
Dimension A difference is material when X versus Y
changes...
Classification which state or status the declared rule permits
Authority who may decide, approve, block, escalate, certify, or
revoke
Permissible action which actions are allowed, required, or prohibited
Execution path which dependency, control, or process path must be
used
Evidence requirement which observations or thresholds must be satisfied
Cost / exposure which resource, risk, liability, or capacity must be
carried
Recovery / rollback which containment, reversal, retry, or escalation is
required
Claim strength what may be asserted as confirmed, probable,
unresolved, or contradicted
3.1 Modality: admissible possibility, not mere conceivability
The governing modality is bounded counterfactual possibility. The question is not whether one
can imagine some remote world in which the distinction matters, and not whether the
distinction has already changed the current outcome. The question is whether, inside the
declared operating envelope and under the declared authority, decision rules, and evidence
rule, at least one admissible case exists in which the two states permit different handling.
Materiality is tested against the system's claimed operating
envelope, not against unlimited imagination.
3.2 Permissible Downstream State
Definition.

A permissible downstream state is a classification, decision, action, escalation, claim, recovery
state, or terminal state allowed by declared authority A under declared decision rules D after
the applicable evidence rule E has returned a state that D maps to that transition, or after D
explicitly permits defer, escalation, or another unresolved path.
Decision rules are not implicit. D is a declared mapping from represented/evidence states to
permitted classifications, actions, claims, escalations, rollback states, recovery states, or
terminal states. A answers who may act; E answers what evidence state exists; D answers what
transition that state permits. The framework treats D as a fifth governing input rather than
hiding it inside authority or evidence.
3.3 The Bounded Materiality Test
1. Declare operation O: what decision, classification, claim, or execution is being supported?
2. Declare authority scope A: who or what is permitted to make, certify, revoke, or escalate the
transition?
3. Declare decision rules D: how do represented states and evidence states map to permitted
classifications, actions, claims, escalations, rollback states, or terminal states?
4. Declare evidence rule E: what observations, thresholds, provenance, recency, contradiction
handling, and authority conditions return each evidence state?
5. Declare operating envelope Omega: which conditions, populations, time horizon, interfaces,
dependencies, and failure classes are inside the system's claimed scope?
6. Enumerate candidate states X and Y and hold unrelated conditions constant.
7. Run X through the same declared tuple (O, A, D, E, Omega) and record the permissible
downstream state set P(X).
8. Run Y through the same declared tuple and record P(Y).
9. Compare the sets. If P(X) differs from P(Y) in any admissible state inside Omega, the
distinction is MATERIAL for O. If the sets are identical across Omega, the distinction is NON-
MATERIAL for O. If O, A, D, E, Omega, X, or Y is insufficiently specified, the result is
UNRESOLVED.
3.4 Materiality Receipt
A materiality claim is receipted by the explicit difference between P(X) and P(Y), together with
the declared operation O, authority A, decision rules D, evidence rule E, and operating envelope
Omega. "Important" is not evidence of materiality. A changed permissible transition under the
declared tuple is.
A distinction is material when the governed system must behave
differently, not merely when an analyst can describe a difference.

4. From External Condition to Representation
Version 1.0 used the shorthand chain "Reality -> observation -> structure -> abstraction -> label."
That shorthand risked implying an unmediated epistemic path back to reality. There is none.
Human and technical systems encounter external conditions through observation channels:
senses, instruments, logs, records, witnesses, tests, interventions, and other interfaces. Every
observation can itself contain error or compression.
The more precise chain is:
External condition -> observation channel(s) -> evidence ->
representation -> claim or decision
Verification does not escape representation. It adds new observation channels and asks whether
the current representation survives discriminating evidence. The return path is therefore
iterative:
Claim -> required evidence -> new observation or intervention ->
comparison -> retain / revise / unresolved
This does not remove epistemic uncertainty. It governs it.
5. Semantic Resolution and Operative Resolution
5.1 Semantic Resolution
Semantic resolution is the amount of operative structure preserved by a representation.
"Vehicle" may be sufficient for deciding whether an object belongs in a parking area and
insufficient for deciding whether it can tow a specified load. "Employed" may be sufficient for a
binary payroll field and insufficient for determining whether a particular employment
arrangement is operationally viable for a household.
5.2 Operative Resolution
Definition.
Operative resolution is the lowest level of abstraction at which every distinction returned
MATERIAL by the Bounded Materiality Test remains representable for the declared operation
and every unresolved materiality question remains explicitly unresolved rather than silently
collapsed.
The stopping rule is therefore external to the phrase "what matters." Further compression is
permitted only while it leaves the permissible downstream state set unchanged across the
declared operating envelope. Further decompression is unnecessary when it does not change
that set and does not resolve an outstanding materiality question.

Compress while the governed transition set stays unchanged.
Decompress when compression changes what the system may do.
Under-decomposition collapses states that require different handling. Over-decomposition
preserves distinctions that do not change the governed transition set for the operation and
therefore adds complexity without increasing operative fidelity.
6. Failure Taxonomy Rebuilt Around Mechanisms
A taxonomy that gives different names to the same mechanism commits the False Difference it
was created to diagnose. The active framework therefore groups failures by root mechanism
and uses an explicit boundary test between Distinction Loss and Representational Substitution.
6.1 Mechanism A: Distinction Loss
Distinction Loss occurs when two or more states that pass the Materiality Test are represented
as equivalent. The specific manifestation depends on what kind of structure was removed.

6.2 Mechanism B: Representational Substitution
Representational Substitution occurs when candidate states remain separately representable,
but evidence or warrant for one state is used to authorize a claim or transition belonging to
another state.
 Process for outcome: workflow completed -> task succeeded.
 Proxy for condition: score improved -> represented condition improved.
 Transport for execution: API returned success -> external state changed.
 Absence of evidence for evidence of absence: no complaint -> no harm.

6.2.1 Boundary Test: Loss versus Substitution
1. Are the candidate states still separately representable at the point of decision? If no, the
failure is Distinction Loss.
2. If yes, is evidence or warrant attached to one state being used to permit a claim or transition
belonging to another? If yes, the failure is Representational Substitution.
3. If neither condition holds, do not force the event into either category; continue
decomposition.
Example: REQUEST_ACCEPTED, EXECUTED, and VALIDATED all becoming DONE is State
Collapse because the states are no longer preserved. Keeping REQUEST_ACCEPTED and
EXECUTED separate but treating a 200 response proving REQUEST_ACCEPTED as proof of
EXECUTED is Substitution because the states remain distinct while warrant crosses the
boundary.
Loss destroys the distinction. Substitution preserves the distinction
but spends proof across it.
6.3 Mechanism C: Persistence and Drift
A representation can remain attached after the underlying condition changes. Semantic
Persistence names the survival of the label; Structural Drift names the growing mismatch
between the retained representation and current evidence. Context Decay is a related case in
which the compressed instruction survives while the shared knowledge required to interpret it
disappears.
6.4 Mechanism D: Chain Integrity Failure
Chain Integrity Failure occurs when a representation is detached from the evidence, conditions,
uncertainty, or transformation history required to interpret it. Chain Severance, provenance
loss, confidence inflation, and confidence attenuation are manifestations of this mechanism.
6.5 Mechanism E: Context Mismatch
A compression can be valid for one receiver and unusable for another because shared context
differs. Compression Mismatch and Decompression Burden arise when the sender and receiver
operate at different semantic resolutions or do not share the same decompression key.
A useful taxonomy groups by mechanism first and names
manifestations second.
7. False Sameness, False Difference, Convergence, and Divergence
False Sameness occurs when one label collapses materially different mechanisms or states.
False Difference occurs when different labels are treated as different mechanisms even though
decompression reveals the same operative chain.

Cross-domain transfer must therefore proceed in two phases: identify structural convergence,
then continue decomposing until a material divergence point is found or the current operation

Do not transfer at the label layer. Match the chain, then search for
the divergence point.
Over-decompression is also possible. If every system is reduced to "state -> transition -> state,"
the representation may become universally true and operationally useless. The correct stopping
point is operative resolution, not metaphysical depth.
8. Semantic Fidelity and Semantic Custody
Earlier versions used overlapping language for fidelity and custody. The active framework
preserves a state-versus-chain boundary and subjects neighboring concepts to the same
admissibility discipline.
8.1 Semantic Fidelity - a state property
Definition.
Semantic fidelity is the degree to which representation B preserves the operation-relevant
distinctions of representation A at a specified comparison point. Fidelity is evaluated between
two states or representations.
A summary can therefore have high topical similarity and low semantic fidelity if it drops a
condition that changes claim strength or action.
8.2 Semantic Custody - a chain property
Definition.
Semantic custody is the controlled preservation and traceability of operation-relevant
distinctions, evidence strength, uncertainty, conditions, and provenance across a sequence of
transformations and handlers.
Custody is not another word for fidelity. Fidelity asks whether meaning survived at a point.
Custody asks whether the system can show how meaning was transformed across the path,
where fidelity was checked, and where degradation occurred.
Fidelity measures the state. Custody governs the path.
8.3 Boundary Test
If the question can be answered by comparing an original and a transformed representation, it
is a fidelity question. If the question requires following multiple transformations, actors, or
evidence handoffs, it is a custody question. If the distinction does not change the analysis, one
term should not be introduced merely to create vocabulary.

9. Reality Primacy Without Unmediated Access
Architectural proposition.
Reality Primacy is an authority rule over representations, not a claim of representation-free
access to the world. External conditions are ontologically prior to their descriptions, while
epistemic access remains mediated through observation channels, instruments, records,
interventions, and other evidence-producing interfaces.
A controlling representation does not lose authority merely because any observer asserts
contradiction. It must, however, enter a governed challenge path when qualifying contradictory
evidence is presented through an authorized validation channel.
Reality gets the last vote, but governance determines when that
vote has been reliably heard.
9.1 Evidence Standard Declaration
Before a consequential transition can be evaluated, the system must declare: the claim being
tested; the candidate states that must be discriminated; mandatory evidence dimensions; the
consequence class; the certifying authority; and any allowed defer/escalation rule. A post-hoc
evidence standard is not an evidence standard; it is retrospective accommodation.
9.2 Evidence Sufficiency Decision Rule
Definition.
Evidence sufficiency is conjunctive unless the governing domain explicitly declares another
combination rule in advance. For the default rule, evidence is SUFFICIENT only when every
mandatory condition below is satisfied:
1. Discrimination: the evidence distinguishes every material candidate state required by the
operation.
2. Provenance: source, collection path, and transformation history meet the declared trust rule.
3. Temporal applicability: the evidence is current enough for the condition and decision horizon
being governed.
4. Contradiction handling: known qualifying contradictions have been resolved, incorporated
into the state model, or explicitly retained as contradictory.
5. Consequence threshold: the declared confidence/evidence threshold for the consequence class
is met.
6. Certification authority: the actor or mechanism assigning the evidence state is authorized to
do so.
The decision rule returns one of four governed states:

Weighted or compensatory evidence rules are permitted only when the domain defines the

9.3 Authorized Reality Challenge Path
The governing chain is: controlling representation -> contradictory observation -> authorized
challenge admission -> Evidence Sufficiency Decision Rule -> evidence state -> authorized
transition.
 SUFFICIENT contradictory evidence: revise, revoke, narrow, or replace the controlling
representation as required by the governing rule.
 UNRESOLVED: move to PENDING_VALIDATION or another explicitly permitted unresolved
state; do not silently preserve conclusive authority.
 CONTRADICTED: escalate, acquire additional discriminating evidence, or narrow the claim
until the contradiction is represented.
 INSUFFICIENT challenge evidence: the prior representation may remain controlling within
its existing validation scope, while the failed challenge and reason remain auditable where
consequence warrants.
9.4 Operational End to the Regress
The verification chain does not terminate in certainty about reality in itself. It terminates
operationally when the declared evidence rule returns a permitted state, the authorized actor
makes the corresponding transition, and residual uncertainty is carried forward at the level
required by that rule. The claim remains reopenable when new qualifying evidence enters the
challenge path.
Reality Primacy grants no observation automatic authority and
grants no prior representation permanent immunity.

10. Epistemic States and Confidence Custody
Known, corroborated, probable, possible, unresolved, contradicted, and pending validation are
different states when they imply different permitted claims or actions. A system should not
flatten them merely to preserve workflow momentum.
Uncertainty is not failure. Hidden uncertainty is failure.
Confidence custody requires that evidentiary strength survive compression. A weak possibility
must not become a confirmed fact through repeated summarization, and strong evidence must
not be diluted into vagueness when that change would alter authority or action.
The paper applies this rule to itself through the Claim Status section. Definitions are not
disguised as measurements; architectural hypotheses are not presented as universal laws;
design rules are conditional on the operation they govern.
11. Authority, Consequence, and Compression Tolerance
Design principle.
Required semantic resolution should increase when collapsing a distinction can increase
consequence. The relationship is not "high stakes always require more words." It is "high-
consequence decisions tolerate fewer unresolved material distinctions."
A label such as APPROVED, DENIED, SAFE, ELIGIBLE, or OPERATIONAL can authorize or block
action. Before such a label controls execution, every distinction that passes the Materiality Test
for that authority path must either be resolved, explicitly deferred under a permitted rule, or
escalated.
11.1 Authority-Relevance Test
A distinction is authority-relevant when resolving it differently would change who may act,
what action is permitted, what evidence is required, what escalation is triggered, or who carries
accountability. Where the test is positive, the authority path must have access to evidence
sufficient to discriminate those states or must be structurally prevented from pretending
resolution.
Formal authority without discriminating evidence is authorized
blindness.
12. Representational Ceilings and Adequacy
Definition.
A representational ceiling is the maximum operative resolution a system can preserve given the
states, relationships, evidence links, uncertainty modes, and authority interfaces available in its
model.

You cannot reason your way out of a distinction your system cannot
represent.
12.1 Representational Ceiling Test
For each distinction returned MATERIAL by the Bounded Materiality Test, determine whether
the system can:
1. represent each candidate state separately;
2. preserve any relation between candidate states whose presence, absence, direction, or value
returns MATERIAL when tested under the same bounded tuple (O, A, D, E, Omega);
Relation materiality uses the same counterfactual boundary as state materiality: compare
relation-present and relation-absent representations while holding unrelated conditions
constant under (O, A, D, E, Omega). If the permissible downstream state set does not change
anywhere inside Omega, preserving that relation is not mandatory for this operation.
3. represent UNRESOLVED or CONTRADICTED where forced certainty would alter the transition;
4. attach the discriminating evidence and provenance required by the Evidence Sufficiency
Decision Rule;
5. expose the resolved or unresolved distinction to the authority that must act on it; and
6. reopen the representation when a declared revalidation trigger occurs.
If any mandatory capability is absent for any material distinction, the representational ceiling is
INADEQUATE for that operation before downstream failure occurs. If every capability is
present, the ceiling is ADEQUATE for the declared operation, not universally adequate.
12.2 Semantic Adequacy Test
A representation is semantically adequate for an intended operation when the Representational
Ceiling Test returns ADEQUATE, the current evidence state is permitted by the governing rule,
and the resulting representation supports the required action, validation, recovery, and
revalidation paths without silently collapsing a material unresolved state.
Data volume is not representational adequacy. Precision cannot
rescue a missing state.
13. Metrics, Proxies, and Representational Gaming
A proxy becomes dangerous when proof that the proxy was satisfied is substituted for proof that
the represented condition occurred. This is Representational Substitution combined with
execution authority.
A metric can be precise while its category is crude. A system can therefore optimize a number
while the condition it was meant to represent remains unchanged or deteriorates.

Architectural hypothesis.
When a compressed indicator becomes a target and the underlying condition is not
independently validated, systems create incentives to produce the representation rather than
the represented condition. The strength of this effect depends on the incentive structure,
observability, and cost of external validation.
Producing the representation is not necessarily producing the
represented condition.
14. Compression Stacks and Recovery Paths
Large systems require nested abstraction. Raw events become transaction states. Transaction
states become daily rates. Rates become KPIs. KPIs become dashboards. Dashboards become
narratives. Each transformation may be legitimate while still removing structure.
A governed compression stack therefore needs transformation criteria, provenance, confidence
custody, exception visibility, and a recovery path to the evidence beneath consequential
abstractions.
Every consequential abstraction should preserve an auditable path
to the evidence that justified it.
A receipt is one form of this path. "Execution succeeded" is the compressed claim. The receipt
preserves request, authority, target, time, state transition, observed result, validation, and any
remaining uncertainty.
15. Intelligence as Governed Compression
Architectural hypothesis.
Reasoning, learning, understanding, memory, adaptation, judgment, and observation are
frequently compressed under intelligence. Decompression may reveal shared chains and deeper
divergence. A learning architecture that cannot refine its own distinctions is limited in how it
can respond when evidence repeatedly violates its current categories.
15.1 Representational Refinement
Definition.
Representational refinement is the evidence-driven splitting, merging, or restructuring of
internal categories when the current representation fails the Materiality Test for the operation
the system is trying to perform.
This does not imply that every learner must rewrite its ontology in the same way. It makes a
narrower claim: where two observed states require different justified handling, a learner must

16. Compression Governance
Compression becomes governable only when the transformation has declared scope, custody,
expiration, and violation handling. A descriptive schema is not enough.
16.1 Compression Contract
Definition.
A Compression Contract is an executable governance record defining: source state; permitted
compressed state; intended operation; authority owner; Bounded Materiality Test result;
evidence standard; retained provenance; prohibited downstream uses; expiration or
revalidation trigger; recovery path; and violation response.

16.3 Violation Response
A contract must declare at least one enforceable response for REVALIDATION_REQUIRED,
EXPIRED, or VIOLATED states: block, quarantine, rollback, retry, escalate, narrow the claim, or
acquire renewed evidence. "Should reopen" is insufficient because it describes preference
without controlling execution.
A compression contract governs only when breaking it changes
what the system is allowed to do.

17. Semantic Decompression Protocol v1.4
1. Declare operation O, authority A, decision rules D, evidence rule E, and operating envelope
Omega.
2. Identify the compressed term, metric, category, or state controlling the operation.
3. Enumerate plausible candidate states beneath the compression.
4. Run the Bounded Materiality Test; retain MATERIAL and UNRESOLVED distinctions, discard
only NON-MATERIAL distinctions for this operation.
5. Set operative resolution at the lowest level that preserves those distinctions.
6. Run the Representational Ceiling Test. If INADEQUATE, block the stronger claim or redesign
the representation before proceeding.
7. Declare the evidence standard and run the Evidence Sufficiency Decision Rule.
8. Return SUFFICIENT, INSUFFICIENT, UNRESOLVED, or CONTRADICTED; do not force a
terminal state that the evidence rule does not permit.
9. Execute only a permissible downstream transition under the declared authority.
10. Observe the external result and capture a receipt linking action, evidence, outcome, and
provenance.
11. Apply the Compression Contract, including expiration, revalidation triggers, and violation
response.
12. Reopen the representation when new qualifying evidence or contract state requires it.
A protocol is operational only when its tests return states that
constrain execution.

23. Adversarial Execution III - Ambiguous Revalidation Trigger
Case: A Compression Contract says a state must reopen when conditions "materially change."
The trigger itself is semantically compressed and therefore cannot control execution.
1. Failure: "materially change" supplies no observable event, threshold, or authority. The
contract cannot determine when VALID becomes REVALIDATION_REQUIRED.
2. Repair: replace the phrase with explicit triggers appropriate to the operation, for example:
evidence age exceeds 24 hours; dependency version changes; authority owner changes;
measured error rate crosses 2 percent for two consecutive windows; contradictory qualifying
evidence is admitted; or the operating envelope changes.

through the named authority to REVALIDATION_REQUIRED. Dependent consequential actions
are blocked, deferred, or narrowed according to the violation/revalidation rule.

24. Adversarial Execution IV - Evidence Sufficiency Conflict

27. Testable Design Rules for Systems
The following are conditional design rules. Each states an activation condition and a governed
response.
 If O, A, D, E, or Omega is undeclared, a materiality result is UNRESOLVED; consequential
compression may not rely on a presumed materiality judgment.
 If P(X) differs from P(Y) inside the declared operating envelope under the same (O, A, D, E,
Omega), X/Y is MATERIAL and the distinction must remain representable, explicitly
deferred, or blocked from governing action.
 If a material distinction fails any mandatory capability in the Representational Ceiling Test,
the representation is INADEQUATE for that operation and the stronger transition is
prohibited until redesigned or narrowed.
 If any mandatory Evidence Sufficiency condition fails, SUFFICIENT is prohibited; the system
must return INSUFFICIENT, UNRESOLVED, or CONTRADICTED as appropriate.
 If candidate states remain separate but evidence for one is used to authorize another,
classify the failure as Representational Substitution; if the states are no longer separately
representable, classify it as Distinction Loss.

28. Conclusion
Semantic compression is a state-management problem for understanding, authority, and
execution. Every functioning human and technical system compresses. The governing question
is not whether compression exists, but whether the distinctions removed by that compression
can change what the system is permitted to claim or do.
Version 1.4 closes the structural defects identified in adversarial review of v1.3. Active
framework sections no longer describe a prior version as the acting subject, and the revision
performs a recurrence sweep so that version-attribution repair is not local to a single section.
The revision also places internal certification under the same Compression Contract machinery
required elsewhere in the framework. The certification now has an explicit source state,
permitted compressed state, scope, owner, validity state, expiration conditions, observable
revalidation triggers, prohibited uses, and violation response. A positive status may persist only
while that contract remains VALID.
The resulting dependency chain is explicit: declared operation -> authority scope -> decision
rules -> operating envelope -> candidate states -> bounded materiality -> operative resolution ->
representational ceiling -> evidence rule -> evidence sufficiency state -> permissible transition ->
compression contract -> execution -> observation -> revalidation.
The framework passes only when its own terms survive the tests it
requires other systems to survive.
Reality remains larger than every representation. The framework therefore does not promise
final certainty. It governs the authority of claims under bounded evidence, preserves
unresolved and contradictory states when required, and keeps every consequential
representation reopenable to qualifying evidence.
