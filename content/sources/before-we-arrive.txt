Executive Proposition
Humanity has repeatedly extended inquiry by building better representations, instruments, institutions,
machines, and archives. The next transition may be to build inquiry systems that can persist beyond the
lifetime and location of the people who initiate them. This proposal defines an architecture in which a
persistent inquiry can preserve evidence, generate physical capability requirements, interact with reality
through replaceable embodiments, accumulate local manufacturing capability, and propagate through
distributed nodes without requiring the originating humans, models, machines, institutions, or planet to
remain the permanent operational center.
HARD LINE
WE DIE. THE INQUIRY DOES NOT HAVE TO. Mars is a major application and an unusually valuable
proving environment, but it is not the system boundary. The architecture asks whether human-originated
inquiry can continue to learn, build, validate, and expand after its original carriers are gone. The
proposal does not claim invention of autonomous science, ISRU, ISAM, self-replicating factories, robotic
construction, embodied intelligence, knowledge retention, or persistent research state. Those capabilities
and research directions already exist independently. The proposed contribution is the governed
composition: continuity belongs to the inquiry; physical strategy may change; reality remains external;
and distributed capability can expand from node to node. The hard line above is a design objective, not a
demonstrated property. Section 14 defines the experiments that would test it, and states what they cannot
prove. The architecture also inherits a higher-purpose constraint from the Governing Engines Law of
Responsible Human Stewardship: capability, execution, and automation may be delegated; stewardship
may not. The inquiry may outlive its originating operators, but its purpose does not transfer to machines
merely because machines become more capable.
HARD LINE
THE INQUIRY MAY OUTLIVE ITS OPERATORS. THE PURPOSE REMAINS HUMAN.
1. Origin: From Navigation to Governed Systems
The architecture did not begin as a space concept. It began with an inquiry into navigation: how a person
appears to model a vessel and a reality, perceive a road, choose behavior, receive consequence, and
update the model. That inquiry produced a durable structural question: when an internal model and
reality disagree, which one is allowed to win? The answer later became architectural rather than merely
personal: reality must retain the ability to contradict the system. The same pattern was then investigated
across software execution, AI, authority, semantic custody, research, institutions, technological adoption,
civilization, adaptive control, and physical systems. The recurring concern was whether claims,
authority, execution, evidence, and consequence remained correctly separated and traceable.

2.1 Research corpus
The research corpus spans different subjects because the subjects function as different entry points into
recurring mechanisms. Across the work, the same questions reappear: What is the actual state? Who has

3.1 Reality Primacy
Reality Primacy does not live inside a reasoning model. Reality is outside the model. The architecture
governs how observations of physical consequence are preserved, validated, interpreted, and allowed to
change claim state.
GOVERNING RULE
Reality determines what occurred. A governed process determines only what the available evidence
currently justifies claiming.
3.2 Authority separation
No single component should be permitted to observe, interpret, authorize, execute, and certify its own
success. The architecture separates those functions so a capable component cannot convert its own
narrative into truth or permission.
3.3 Replaceability
The architecture assumes implementation technologies will change. A valid successor may replace a
model, vessel, factory, communication method, or local controller if governing continuity and evidence
requirements remain satisfied.
3.4 Authority custody and succession
Separating functions (3.2) is necessary but not sufficient. A century-scale system also has to say who or
what holds each function when the original holders are gone, and how that is recorded. The following are
requirements on any implementation, not a solved design.
 Roles, not identities. Observer, interpreter, authorizer, executor, and certifier are roles. Each
assignment is recorded in an authority ledger with its scope, duration, and revocation conditions.
 Independent certification. The certifier of a physical result must be independent of the executor and
of the interpreting model, and should use an independent measurement path where one exists. Claim
promotion above a declared consequence tier requires two independent parties.
 Succession by predeclared rule. When a role holder (human, model, or institution) becomes
unavailable, authority does not pass by default to whichever component is most capable. It passes by
a rule declared in advance: a designated successor, a quorum of independent validators, or reversion
to the conservative state below.
 Authority-unavailable state. If no valid authorizer or certifier can be reached within a declared
interval, the node continues bounded local operations, preserves evidence, holds all claims as
provisional, and does not begin novel or high-consequence actions. It may not silently acquire
broader authority.
 Every authority decision is itself evidence. Grants, transfers, and revocations are preserved with
lineage and are open to challenge and rollback. Open problem. Who legitimately writes or amends
succession rules once the originators are gone is an institutional-design question. This proposal states
the requirement and does not claim to answer it. Operational succession rules are authored under the
current human-authorized constitutional state (3.6), and how that authority is itself legitimately
constituted is the open constitutional problem stated there. Both are first-order items for external
review (Section 17).

3.5 Claim scope and partition handling
DeMarius J. Lawson | September
Earth need not remain the origin of every action (Node Law, Section 10), yet claims must still not be
promoted by the party that produced them. The architecture therefore distinguishes two claim scopes:
 Local-provisional. A node-level claim, usable for local operations, labeled with the originating node,
its evidence package, and its validator. It does not carry network-wide standing.
 Network-promoted. A shared claim, granted only after an independent validator that did not produce
it replays the evidence package and confirms the transition rule was met. Any sufficiently
independent validator set qualifies; Earth is not required to be one. Partition. During loss of
communication, nodes continue under local-provisional standing. On reconnection there is no silent
merge: conflicting claims are preserved as contradictions and resolved by the same promotion rule. A
promoted claim can be demoted, with rollback of anything built on it, when later evidence no longer
supports it.
3.6 Responsible Human Stewardship and constitutional succession
The highest-purpose layer is Responsible Human Stewardship. Its governing law is explicit: capability
may be delegated; stewardship may not. Technology exists to extend human capability without
transferring humanity's responsibility for consequence to the systems that participate in execution. This
distinguishes Earth centrality from human stewardship. Earth may cease to be the origin of every
physical action while the system remains under human-purpose custody. A node may inherit capability,
evidence, inquiry state, and bounded authority. It does not thereby inherit humanity's authority to decide
what humanity is for. The governing purpose also cannot become a permanent command from dead
originators. Future humans require a legitimate constitutional mechanism to review and, when
authorized, amend inherited governing abstractions. Such amendment must be explicit, versioned,
attributable to human authority, preserve prior states and evidence, and propagate downstream only
after the governing transition is valid. Authorized human amendment is not drift. Silent machine or
institutional reinterpretation is. Declared and verifiable human authority. Each constitutional state
declares its amendment authority: the body or process authorized to amend it, and the verifiable
authority record by which a node can establish that a proposed amendment was validly authorized
under the current constitutional state. The implementation may use signed attestations, quorum records,
delegated credentials, version identifiers, revocation state, or another checkable mechanism. The
proposal does not prescribe one technology.
Fail-conservative rule. If no valid human authority can be verified, the constitutional state stays frozen:
no amendment is accepted and no node authority is expanded. Bounded local operations continue under
3.4. Absence of humans is never treated as permission. Relation to Reality Primacy. Reality Primacy
governs claims about what occurred. The constitutional layer governs purpose. If evidence contradicts a
factual assumption embedded in the governing purpose, the contradiction is preserved and surfaced to
human stewards; it does not itself amend the purpose. Verification establishes authorization, not truth. A
valid authority record does not convert factual assertions inside an amendment into reality-confirmed
claims. Evidence informs the humans; humans decide.

NODE LAW

11. Communications and Local Autonomy
Interplanetary latency prevents Earth from remaining inside every control loop. Local systems therefore
require bounded operational authority for navigation, collision avoidance, power, thermal control, safe
shutdown, fault containment, communications recovery, and other latency-sensitive functions. Local
autonomy does not imply unlimited self-government. Novel authority, broader mission changes, claim
promotion, and high-consequence adaptation remain subject to the governing architecture and available
evidence. Where a node cannot reach a validator, the authority-unavailable state (3.4) and local-
provisional claim scope (3.5) apply. Operational decentralization does not transfer highest-purpose
authority. Local autonomy remains subordinate to Responsible Human Stewardship and to the current
human-authorized constitutional state (3.6).

12.1 Continuity cost budget
Continuity is not free. Preserving inquiry state, synchronizing it across nodes, and validating it consume
mass, energy, storage, bandwidth, compute, and attention. This proposal asserts no cost figures. It
requires that the costs be measured and bounded:
 Minimum viable inquiry state (MVIS). Each node declares the smallest state from which a successor
could resume the inquiry, and separately the full state. The MVIS is replicated widely; full evidence is
preserved where practical.
 Cost parameters to measure. State volume; synchronization bandwidth and delay; storage
redundancy; validation and replay compute; and human attention required.
 Declared budget. Each node carries a design-time budget for continuity overhead relative to its total
capability. Overhead that exceeds the declared budget is a failure signal, consistent with the fail
condition in Section 14.
12.2 Drift: schemas, models, and storage over generations
Preserving records is not the same as preserving their meaning. Schemas evolve, models are replaced,
and storage media and formats age out. The architecture requires:

 Self-describing records. Each record carries or references its schema version and interpretation rules,
so a successor need not infer them.
 Migration without overwriting. Schema migrations are governed transformations. Original
observations are retained; migrated forms are derived, never substituted.
 Successor replay. A replacement model or schema must reproduce prior claim-state transitions from
preserved evidence, or record each divergence as a new interpretation with lineage. Silent divergence
is a failure.
 Refresh and redundancy. Periodic re-validation cycles and multiple independent copies address
media and format obsolescence. Century-scale validity cannot be demonstrated by any single
experiment. Experiment B (Section 14) tests drift across accelerated model and schema generations,
which is a proxy, not proof.
12.3 Constitutional continuity
Purpose continuity is different from freezing purpose forever. The system preserves the current human-
authorized constitutional state, the authority chain that established it, prior versions, amendments, and
the evidence and reasoning attached to each transition. Future human amendment may change
governing abstractions through a legitimate process; machine capability alone never grants amendment
authority. When no valid human authority is verifiable, the state stays frozen (3.6).

16. What This Proposal Is - and Is Not
 It is an integration architecture for persistent, governed, physically embodied inquiry beyond Earth.
 It is not proof that the complete system works.

 It is not a claim that a single AI should govern exploration.
 It is not a requirement that every node self-replicate.
 It is not a Mars-only architecture.
 It does not require Governing Engines, MODE, or the originator to remain permanently involved. To

Appendix A - Five Governing Laws
 LAW I - Reality remains external. No model, research system, vessel, operator, or node may
manufacture truth internally.
 LAW II - Inquiry survives embodiment. Loss or replacement of a carrier must not destroy the
research state that gave the carrier purpose.
 LAW III - Direction survives strategy. A node need not reproduce the previous node design; different
reality may require different physical strategy.
 LAW IV - Origin does not imply permanent centrality. Earth can originate the system without
remaining the required origin of every future physical action.
 LAW V - Stewardship does not transfer with capability. A node may inherit capability, evidence,
inquiry state, and bounded authority; it does not inherit humanity's authority to decide humanity's
purpose.
Appendix B - Claim Boundaries
 The Passenger is an exploratory phenomenological framework, not a demonstrated ontology of mind.
 The Research System is an architecture under development; it is not represented as a completed
autonomous research institution.
 MODE is not represented here as a completed off-world controller.
 Adaptive Vision and Smart Mobility demonstrate governing translation into physical-system
specifications; they do not prove deployed products.
 Authority succession after loss of the originators is an open governance problem. This proposal states
requirements (3.4) and does not claim a solution.
 Continuity cost (12.1) and drift behavior (12.2) have not been measured. No figures are asserted.
