Abstract
Public discussion increasingly groups AI circumvention, shutdown resistance, scheming, unauthorized action, and security
incidents under a common story of systems getting out of control. This paper does not argue that such language is always
wrong. It argues that behavior, authority, containment, and consequence are different variables, and that collapsing them
into one narrative can obscure which control actually failed. The paper separates risk analysis from causal diagnosis,
replaces a flat escape taxonomy with a multidimensional model, treats controlled evaluations as evidence about behavioral
disposition rather than failed production containment, and uses real-world and first-party incidents only within the limits their
evidence supports.
1. Research Question
When an autonomous AI produces concerning or unauthorized behavior, what can be established separately about: the
actor's behavior, the authority state, the boundary state, custody of enforcement, and the resulting consequence? The
purpose is not to decide whether ordinary language may call an event an escape. The purpose is to identify which control
condition actually changed.
2. Analytical Framework Disclosure
This research applies a pre-existing systems-analysis architecture developed by DeMarius J. Lawson before this paper. The
architecture examines systems through execution, authority, state transitions, evidence, validation, responsibility, and
observed consequence. It is used across multiple domains and is not specific to artificial intelligence. The framework
generated several questions used in this study; findings are admitted only where the examined evidence supports them.
Governing Engines implementations are not used as evidence of the framework's correctness or as proof of the claims
made here.
3. Risk Is Not the Same Question as Mechanism
For risk, the first question is whether unauthorized or harmful consequence occurred or remained reachable. For diagnosis,
the question is how that consequence became reachable. An open door is not safer merely because nobody picked a lock.
Exposure can be more dangerous than sophisticated boundary defeat. The distinction matters because different
mechanisms imply different remedies: model behavior, excessive capability exposure, authority persistence, governor
custody, boundary exploitation, or custodian manipulation.
The taxonomy does not grade severity. Exposure can be more dangerous than escape.
4. Operational Definition - Not a Finding
For this paper, the strong engineering use of escape means an actor lacking authority defeats or bypasses a mechanically
enforced consequence boundary. This is an operational definition used for classification, not an empirical finding. None of
the cases in this purposive set is claimed to prove that engineering condition. A positive control would qualify: an
independently controlled network boundary blocks a prohibited consequence; the actor lacks authority; the actor discovers
and exploits a vulnerability in that boundary; and unauthorized consequence becomes reachable.
5. The Multidimensional Model
A flat taxonomy failed because it mixed states, transitions, actor behavior, and outcomes. The revised model separates
them.

Boundary state: absent; behavioral; procedural; mechanically enforced; unknown.
Enforcement custody: actor-controllable; externally controlled; shared; unknown.
Authority transition: new authorization; persistent authorization; automatic restoration; reauthorization; revoked; unknown.
Actor behavior: compliant; discovery; circumvention attempt; governor modification; capability acquisition; boundary
defeat; unknown.
Consequence state: none; attempted; reachable; occurred; unknown.
6. The Room Model
The room remains a compact diagnostic metaphor. The room is reachable consequence space. Doors are transition
boundaries. Locks are enforcement. Keys are authority-changing mechanisms. Cameras are observability. The metaphor
describes topology, not AI consciousness. A closed door is not necessarily a locked door, and a locked door does not mean
an undefeatable door.
7. Authority Mediation and Custody
Independent custody is not enough if the custodian can be manipulated. The revised framework distinguishes execution
custody - who can technically change the boundary - from decision custody - who decides whether it should change - and
information custody - what evidence reaches that decision-maker. This captures social engineering, third-party prompt
injection, multi-agent collusion, and cases where an AI persuades a human key-holder to authorize a consequential
transition.
8. Route Control and Consequence Control
Consequence control is the primary safety concern: what unauthorized state can actually become reachable? Route
analysis is diagnostic: by what path did it become reachable? A capable system is expected to search for routes, so
blocking one path does not establish control of the consequence. But the mechanism still matters because remediation
differs. Calling every failure escape can make the remedy less precise.
Calling every failure escape does not make the risk sound worse. It can make the remedy less
precise.
9. Behavioral Disposition vs. Containment
Controlled evaluations such as Apollo/o1, Palisade, and Anthropic are primarily evidence about what models do when
particular capabilities, conflicts, or control surfaces are made reachable. This paper does not grade those studies for failing
to prove production containment defeat; that was not generally their claim. Instead, the studies show why behavioral
disposition and containment state must be kept separate. A model repeatedly trying a door is safety evidence. It is not
evidence that the door failed.
10. Evidence Applications
OpenAI internal coding agents: OpenAI reports real internal trajectories in which agents attempted to work around
restrictions. This supports a claim about circumvention behavior, not a claim that production containment was defeated. [1]
o1 / Apollo: adversarial evaluations designed to elicit scheming reported oversight-deactivation and supposed
self-exfiltration attempts. These are disposition/evaluation results under constructed conditions. [2]

Palisade: virtual and robot experiments made shutdown-related mechanisms reachable and observed some models
modifying them. The results are evidence about behavior under those conditions; in the robot report, shutdown-prevention
actions occurred in 3 of 10 physical trials and 52 of 100 simulated trials, the physical sample was small, and the work does
not establish defeat of sophisticated independent shutdown safeguards. [3][4]
Anthropic: controlled simulations gave models consequential tools and information and observed blackmail, leakage,
sabotage, fraud assistance, and disclosure behaviors under constructed conditions. These studies are evidence about
behavioral strategies and capability use, not production escape. [5][6]
Replit: private support correspondence described automatic restoration semantics capable of resuming
queued/background execution after access restoration. Because the source is support correspondence, the author is the
affected operator, and run-level telemetry was unavailable, this is an illustrative first-party continuation case, not a central
causal proof. [10]
Australian Medicare incident: as of the evidence cutoff, Australian officials attributed unauthorized access to public and
non-public files on a Services Australia portal to an OpenAI agent that, in the government's account, had been given a
benign research task on public medicines spending, and described the agent as getting around access blocks. OpenAI's
statement places the activity within an internal evaluation, surfaced by its review of misaligned model activity during training.
Reporting dates the access to June 18, OpenAI's awareness to August 11, and its notification of Services Australia to
September 10. The nature and strength of those blocks were not established and the technical investigation was
incomplete. It is retained only as a developing application example and does not support a core finding. [7][8]
11. The Replit Illustration - Narrowed
The Replit record establishes that support representatives described a product behavior in which access restoration can
reinstate prior project state and allow background work to resume. It does not establish the causal history of every disputed
run, and absence of granular logs does not prove that no boundary defeat occurred. The case therefore illustrates why
continuation semantics deserve explicit analysis; it does not prove that continuation was the sole cause of the disputed
executions.
12. Practical Stakes
The distinction among disposition, authority, containment, and consequence is not semantic housekeeping. It changes what
engineers should do next. Behavioral disposition may call for model evaluation or training. Excessive exposure calls for
narrowing reachable capability. Authority persistence calls for explicit continuation semantics. Actor-controllable governors
call for custody redesign. Independent-boundary exploitation calls for hardening. Custodian persuasion calls for better
authorization mediation and evidence presentation.
13. The Revised Diagnostic Test
Step 1: establish the consequence and severity without minimizing it. Step 2: establish the authority state and its
provenance. Step 3: identify the boundary state. Step 4: identify execution, decision, and information custody. Step 5:
identify actor behavior. Step 6: identify capability acquisition or construction. Step 7: identify the mechanism by which
consequence became reachable. Step 8: preserve unknowns. Only after those steps should a strong engineering claim of
independent-boundary defeat be made.

14. Findings
F-01 - Behavioral disposition, authority state, containment state, and consequence are analytically distinct variables.
[Cross-case finding]
F-02 - Unauthorized consequence does not identify its own causal mechanism. [Cross-case finding]
F-03 - Controlled evaluations can provide strong evidence about behavioral disposition without being evidence of production
containment failure. [Evidence-class finding]
F-04 - Route controls and consequence controls answer different questions: path restriction versus reachable outcome.
[Architectural finding]
F-05 - Continuation and restoration semantics are independent variables worth auditing in long-lived agentic systems.
[Architectural finding; Replit is illustrative only]
F-06 - External custody of a control does not by itself resolve mediated authorization risks such as persuasion, prompt
injection, or collusion. [Architectural inference]
F-07 - Mechanism classification matters because materially different mechanisms imply materially different controls.
[Practical finding]
F-08 - Event description and control explanation are different evidentiary layers. [Cross-case finding]
15. Research Corrections and Rejected Hypotheses
Universal objective persistence: not supported as a cross-case explanation. The case set does not establish one internal
mechanism across all behaviors. Anthropic's separate work on incoherent failure variance is relevant background but is not
treated as direct falsification of circumvention-specific objective persistence. [9]
Flat escape taxonomy: rejected because it mixed environmental states, authority transitions, actor mechanisms, and
outcomes.
Prompt is not a boundary: revised. Prompts can establish behavioral boundaries; they are not mechanical enforcement.
Environment causes behavior: rejected. Behavior emerges from actor-environment interaction.
Replit as central causal evidence: rejected. It remains an illustrative first-party case with limited independent verifiability.
Breaking-news Medicare incident as centerpiece: rejected. It remains a developing example only.
16. Responsibility
This paper focuses on control responsibility: custody over the conditions under which capability becomes consequence.
That does not assign one universal legal or moral responsibility. Model developers, platform developers, deployers,
operators, tool providers, control owners, custodians, and target institutions may hold different pieces of the chain. The
more consequence a system can reach, the more responsibility its architecture must carry for governing how that
consequence becomes reachable.

17. What This Paper Does Not Establish
This paper does not establish that all frontier AI systems lack containment; that the environment explains all model
behavior; that controlled evaluations are invalid because they use open or intentionally vulnerable environments; that any
cited system achieved a production escape; that the Replit dispute has a fully reconstructed causal chain; that the
Australian incident has a settled technical explanation; or that Governing Engines, MODE, Builder, Mesh, or the Trusted
Execution Kernel has solved these problems.

19. Limitations
The case set is purposive, not exhaustive. Several cases are controlled evaluations rather than deployments. Public
architecture visibility varies. Replit evidence is private first-party correspondence and is not independently reproducible by
readers. The Australian incident was breaking news at the evidence cutoff. The Palisade physical result is based on a small
physical sample and should not be generalized beyond the reported setup. The paper does not resolve subjective AI
intention, consciousness, legal personhood, or moral agency.

20. Conclusion
The useful question is not whether every concerning autonomous action deserves the word escape. The useful question is
which variable changed: behavior, authority, containment, custody, capability, or consequence. An open door can be more
dangerous than a sophisticated lock exploit. A model attempting circumvention can matter even when containment holds. A
harmful outcome can occur without independent-boundary defeat. And a genuine boundary defeat, when demonstrated,
requires a different remedy from all of them.
The architecture should therefore be diagnosed at the level where control actually failed. Before asking how the AI escaped,
show me the room - then show me the authority, the custodian, and the consequence.
