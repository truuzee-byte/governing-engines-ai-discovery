WHAT IS INTELLIGENCE?
A Reality-Primacy Investigation of Process, Compression, and State Change
DeMarius Lawson — Governing Engines
Version 1.1 — September 2026
Do not test reality against the definition of intelligence. Test the definition of intelligence against reality.
Abstract
Intelligence is usually defined through capacities especially legible in humans: reasoning, learning, problem solving, adaptation, memory, planning, and language. This paper treats that definition as a human representation of an underlying phenomenon rather than as the phenomenon itself. It preserves the investigation as it unfolded: conventional definition, suspicion of abstraction, the rain cycle, process, coupled process, system-boundary failure, memory without language, physical learning, evolution, representation, compression, and a final attempt to define intelligence without making human cognition the reference implementation.
The investigation reaches a working definition: intelligence is the process by which consequential relationships in reality are compressed into operational form and made capable of governing subsequent state change. This is not presented as established fact. It is a bounded hypothesis produced by repeated correction. Under this account, memory is persistent operational compression of prior process; learning is creation or revision of compression through interaction and consequence; reasoning is operation over compressed consequential relationships; prediction is compressed regularity made operational against not-yet-realized conditions; language is portable, high-ratio process compression; and abstraction is lossy compression that can fail when discarded structure later becomes consequential. The paper also supplies an operational test for when persistent structure counts as compression at all, and separates the presence of the candidate architecture from its degree, so that both claims can be checked rather than assumed.
The paper does not claim that Earth is conscious, rocks think, or all causation is intelligence. It asks whether the conventional boundary around intelligence reflects a physical discontinuity in reality or a human classification imposed too high in the stack.
1. The Definition May Be the Problem
The inquiry began with a simple question: what is intelligence? Conventional descriptions cluster around learning, reasoning, understanding, adaptation, problem solving, memory, and the use of knowledge. These descriptions are useful, but they contain a methodological risk. They begin with manifestations of intelligence that humans recognize most easily, especially manifestations humans can report through language, and then treat those manifestations as the phenomenon itself.
A representation is not the thing represented. The word rain is not rain. A map is not the territory. A specification is not implementation. An output is not execution. If intelligence is a real phenomenon, the human definition of intelligence must remain subordinate to whatever process actually exists in reality.
We defined intelligence by looking at ourselves. That does not mean reality did.
Reality Primacy changes the method. Observation produces a candidate explanation. Competing explanations are sought. Existing research enters as evidence, mechanism, contradiction, and comparison. Contradiction forces revision. Only the pattern that survives is retained. A peer-reviewed paper is still a claim about reality; publication makes it inspectable, not infallible.
2. Rain: Removing the Human First
The water cycle became the first specimen because it strips away brains, language, intention, and even life from the center of analysis. Solar radiation contributes to evaporation; atmospheric conditions contribute to condensation; precipitation returns water to land and oceans; runoff, infiltration, storage, and later evaporation continue the transformation. Humans named the stages. Humans did not create the process.
The first correction was recognizing that rain is an event. Evaporation is an event. Condensation is an event. Precipitation is an event. The candidate phenomenon is not one event and not a hidden controller behind the events. It is the organized process through which conditions interact, states transform, and transformed states become conditions for what follows.
The process is the thing.
That changed the unit of analysis. Instead of asking which object possesses intelligence, the investigation began asking which processes instantiate whatever intelligence turns out to be. Humans, animals, plants, AI systems, ecosystems, and planets become environments through which candidate processes occur.
3. Coupled Processes and Boundary Failure
State change alone is too weak. A falling rock changes state. Fire burns. Iron oxidizes. If every transformation is intelligence, intelligence collapses into causation.
Coupling became the first serious differentiator. In a coupled process, the state produced by one process becomes a condition that changes another. In deeper coupling, downstream consequences return and alter the original process or the organization governing future operation. The water cycle is not evaporation alone, condensation alone, or precipitation alone. It exists through coupled transformation.
The Sun then broke the assumed system boundary. Treating solar radiation as an external input to the Earth system assumed a box around Earth before reality justified the box. Follow the process and the line expands through solar radiation, orbital relations, atmosphere, ocean, vegetation, geology, water, and gravity. The same problem appears around organisms.
The boundary of the abstraction is not necessarily the boundary of the phenomenon.
Process-first cognition has serious precedent. Maturana and Varela's autopoietic tradition tied cognition to living organization rather than nervous systems alone.[1] Lyon's review of bacterial cognition examines signal transduction, memory, learning, communication, and information-processing at the cellular scale.[2] Gershenson and colleagues explicitly reinterpret autopoiesis from a process ontology and model it as a self-regenerating reaction network.[3] These sources do not prove the present thesis; they show that process-first accounts are scientifically and philosophically live possibilities.
4. Going Downward
The investigation then moved downward: atom → molecule → reaction → coupled reaction → autocatalytic network → self-maintaining organization. The goal was not to declare chemistry intelligent. It was to find the physical point at which the candidate architecture disappeared.
No clean discontinuity appeared. Organization changes what processes become possible. Reaction products can become conditions for later reactions. Autocatalytic networks can participate in producing conditions of their own continuation. None of this proves intelligence, but it weakens the assumption that intelligence suddenly arrives after a boundary called life.
Physical-learning research sharpens the problem. Stern and Murugan review physical systems that can autonomously adapt parameters through local learning rules without neurons or conventional computational design.[4] Separately, Keim and colleagues review material memories in which physical systems encode, access, and erase signatures of past history, including memories tied to far-from-equilibrium behavior.[10] If learning-like adaptation and memory can be physically instantiated outside nervous systems, neither learning nor memory can safely serve as the boundary unless a deeper physical distinction is demonstrated.
5. Memory Without Testimony
Memory initially looked like a stronger boundary. Humans can report remembering. Animals can be tested behaviorally. Plants, materials, ecosystems, and planetary systems cannot testify in human language. But testimony is not direct access to memory. It is another representation produced by the process.
Absence of a human-readable representation is not evidence of absence of the represented process.
The operational question is simpler: does prior history leave a persistent difference that changes subsequent operation? If the same present condition produces systematically different future operation because prior history differed, something about that history remained consequential.
Memory is persistence of consequential difference across time.
This permits comparison without pretending equivalence. Neural plasticity, immune adaptation, material hysteresis, trained model parameters, developmental state, and ecological history can all be examined through the same question: did earlier process leave persistent organization that becomes operational later?
6. Language Is Compression
The word itself became the next clue. RAIN compresses an enormous physical process into a portable state. The word can alter behavior when rain is absent, distant, remembered, future, or hypothetical. Language therefore allows one process to make another consequential without requiring the represented process to recur.
A word is a compression of a process.
Language is not intelligence itself under this account. It is a highly developed compression mechanism used by intelligent process. Its power is portability and reuse. Its danger is distortion. The compressed representation can continue governing action after it no longer preserves something consequential about the process it represents.
7. Compression
Compression became the first candidate mechanism that explained several manifestations of intelligence rather than merely listing them. Rate-distortion theory formalizes a tradeoff between compression and distortion, and Sims applies that framework to human perception under capacity constraints.[5] Minimum Description Length provides a separate formal tradition connecting inductive inference, model selection, prediction, and compression.[8] These literatures do not establish the paper's definition of intelligence; they establish that compression can be formalized rather than left as metaphor.
Memory compresses prior interaction into present organization. Concepts compress many encounters into categories. DNA embodies consequences of evolutionary history without replaying that history. A trained AI model embodies consequences of training in its parameters. Language compresses processes into portable symbols.
Compression alone remains too broad. Geological structure embodies history. A crystal lattice constrains later growth. A photograph preserves aspects of a scene. The next requirement is operational reuse: the compressed consequence must participate causally in subsequent process.
The original process does not have to recur in full for its consequential structure to matter again.
8. An Operational Test for Compression
Compression has been used loosely to this point — useful for exposition, insufficient for falsification. Two systems can both carry structure from their history while only one exercises the candidate architecture. What is needed is a test a critic could apply without first assuming the conclusion.
The test has two independent conditions. Both must hold.
Condition 1 — Lossy reduction. The persistent structure, call it C, must be reachable from more than one distinct antecedent process. If the mapping from history to C is close to one-to-one — if C preserves enough information to reconstruct its generating history in full — then C is a copy, not a compression, however useful it remains. Compression requires that something was discarded: distinct histories collapse toward a shared operational structure. This condition follows the same logic as the rate-distortion and minimum-description-length literature already cited in Section 7: compression is a many-to-one mapping, not merely a change of format.
Condition 2 — Causal parameterization. C must function as a parameter that a later process, B, reads, in the sense that deliberately varying C, while holding the rest of B's initial conditions fixed, produces a systematic, non-incidental change in B's trajectory. This is the difference between C merely being co-present with B and C actively governing some portion of B's behavior. A structure that exists but is never causally load-bearing for any later process fails this condition, regardless of how much history it encodes.
Both conditions are required because each alone is too permissive. Condition 1 alone would admit any noisy or degraded signal; thermal noise loses information about its source without being intelligence. Condition 2 alone would admit ordinary rigid causation; a lever is causally load-bearing for whatever it moves without having compressed anything.
Applying both conditions to the cases already raised in this paper narrows rather than widens the earlier intuitions:
A rock's shape satisfies Condition 1: many distinct erosion histories converge on similar shapes. It satisfies Condition 2: the shape parameterizes the trajectory of whatever strikes or flows around it. The architecture is present in extremely shallow form — the conclusion Section 15 reaches independently, now on a stated test rather than intuition.
A crystal lattice satisfies both conditions for the same reason: distinct growth conditions converge on similar lattice structure, and the lattice parameterizes subsequent crystal growth and fracture behavior. Section 7's earlier treatment of the crystal as an example that merely "embodies history" without qualifying was premature. Under this test the crystal belongs beside the rock, at the same shallow tier, not outside the architecture.
A photograph satisfies Condition 1 strongly — a two-dimensional light-intensity array is a lossy projection of a much richer scene — but fails Condition 2 while unobserved: nothing in the photograph's own subsequent process reads it as a parameter. It becomes operational only inside whatever system later interprets it. The photograph is not itself the intelligent process; the interpreting system is. This is the more precise reason Section 7 was right to treat the photograph differently from memory: not because it lacks compression, but because compression alone, without a downstream reader, never satisfies Condition 2.
DNA, trained model parameters, and synaptic weights satisfy both conditions robustly: each is reachable from many distinct histories — mutation and selection, training runs, experience — that converge toward similar functional structure, and each is continuously read as a governing parameter by ongoing biological or computational process.
This test does not resolve every boundary case, and it is not meant to. It gives a critic something to apply rather than something to accept, which is the requirement Section 21 sets for the paper as a whole.
9. Abstraction as Lossy Compression
Section 8 gave a test for when persistent structure counts as compression at all. This section turns to a distinct problem: even structure that passes that test remains fallible.
Compression necessarily creates a fidelity problem. Some structure is discarded. A metric compresses organizational reality. A financial model compresses economic relationships. A law compresses governance intent. A theory compresses observations. A specification compresses desired behavior. An AI model compresses patterns from training data.
The problem is not compression itself. Compression may be necessary for intelligence. Failure occurs when discarded structure becomes consequential while the compressed representation continues governing subsequent state change as though fidelity were intact.
Abstraction failure is compression distortion becoming operational.
Reality Primacy can therefore be understood as compression validation: does the operational representation still preserve the consequential structure required for the next action? When evidence contradicts the compression, the compression must yield.
10. Learning, Reasoning, Prediction, and Error
Once compression is central, familiar faculties can be redescribed without making them the definition of intelligence. Learning is creation or revision of compression through interaction and consequence. Reasoning is operation over compressed consequential relationships. Prediction is compressed regularity from prior process made operational against conditions that have not yet occurred. Memory is persistent operational compression. Language is transferable compression.
Error appears when a compression can diverge from the process it represents. Direct physical interaction produces consequence. A representation can remain wrong while continuing to govern action. The more process is mediated through compression, the greater the reach of intelligence and the greater the possibility of abstraction drift.
A physical explanation of intelligence does not explain intelligence away.
11. Evolution: Reality as Selection
Evolution provides a severe test because it retains consequential structure without intention. Variation interacts with reality. Consequences differ. Persistence and reproduction differ. Population organization changes. The process repeats. Present biological organization therefore embodies consequences of historical interaction without requiring the historical process to recur.
Watson and Szathmáry review formal correspondences between evolutionary processes and learning, including links to Bayesian learning, neural-network learning, and distributed memory models.[6] Their proposal remains an argument about how far those correspondences can be taken, not proof that evolution and cognition are identical. That distinction is useful: it removes intention as an automatic boundary while preventing analogy from becoming proof.
Evolution also shows that compression is not automatically truth. Adaptations can be local, path-dependent, stale, or fitted to conditions that later disappear. Intelligence therefore cannot mean correctness. It can produce compressions that work under one reality and fail under another.
A further possibility follows. Evolution tests variants physically across generations. Nervous systems can test some variants within a lifetime. Human reasoning can simulate alternatives before physical execution. Language can transfer compressed experience so another person does not repeat the original trial. Science aggregates compression across generations. AI can operationalize enormous historical compression at machine speed. Increasing intelligence may therefore include increasing ability to compress the cost of search.
12. AI as a Clean Comparison
AI makes the compression architecture unusually visible. Training exposes a model to enormous historical process. The trained parameter organization is not a replay of that history; it is a compressed consequence of it. Inference operationalizes that compression against new input.
A fixed model can exercise compressed history without revising itself during the interaction. An adaptive system adds another loop: compression → operation → consequence → compression revision → changed operation. This is structurally close to the Reality Primacy principle that evidence may change understanding and changed understanding must change subsequent reasoning.
The comparison also separates intelligence from execution. A model can exercise intelligence in generating an operational compression without being authorized to force external state change. Intelligence and execution authority are different questions.
13. Plants, Animals, and the Language Trap
Animals already force humans to infer internal process from behavior rather than testimony. Plants push the problem further because they lack animal nervous systems while exhibiting complex sensing, signaling, regulation, developmental history, and environment-dependent response. The plant-cognition debate is valuable because it exposes assumptions on both sides.
If a critic explains plant behavior through biochemical mechanism, that explanation does not automatically disprove intelligence. Human cognition is also physically implemented. At the same time, strong claims about plant consciousness are actively contested; Taiz and colleagues argue that evidence for consciousness, cognition, intentionality, emotion, and pain in plants is highly problematic and emphasize the absence of animal-like neural organization.[11] The present paper does not settle that dispute by vocabulary. The question is whether the process architecture differs in a way that matters to the definition.
The operational test remains the same: does prior process leave compressed consequential structure that later becomes active in governing state change? If yes, the process meets the current candidate definition regardless of whether humans can interrogate it through language.
14. Earth as a Boundary Test
Planetary-intelligence research already challenges individual-only framing. Frank, Grinspoon, and Walker explicitly define planetary intelligence as collective knowledge operating at planetary scale and integrated into coupled planetary systems.[7] Their thesis is not identical to this one; it largely concerns collective biological and technological intelligence becoming planetary in scale.
The present inquiry asks something underneath that: if planetary and ecological history becomes compressed into persistent organization and that organization later governs consequential process, why should the process be excluded solely because no singular planetary speaker can report an internal state?
This does not establish a conscious Earth or planetary mind. Consciousness is not part of the working definition. The claim is narrower: some Earth processes may instantiate the same compression-operationalization architecture observed in systems conventionally called intelligent.
15. The Rock Problem
The rock is the necessary embarrassment. Geological processes produce present structure, and present structure changes future interaction. Under a careless definition, the rock becomes intelligent and the concept appears absurd.
The correction is to stop classifying the object. The unit is the process. Section 8's operational test settles the question without appeal to intuition: a rock's shape is reachable from many distinct erosion histories (Condition 1) and parameterizes the trajectory of whatever strikes or flows around it (Condition 2). Both conditions hold, so the rock exhibits the candidate architecture — in extremely shallow form, with effectively no recursive depth and narrow operational reach (Section 17). Calling this "intelligence" will strike most readers as a stretch of the word. That discomfort is a fact about the word's history, not a refutation of the test; Section 17 gives the vocabulary to say a rock has the architecture and almost none of the degree.
This is where intelligence separates from simple causation. A → B is state change. The candidate architecture is A → persistent reduced structure C → A no longer needs to recur → C later changes B because consequential structure from A survived in operational form.
16. Working Definition
At this point the investigation is deliberately frozen. Continuing into replication, consciousness, cosmology, and every adjacent question would allow the inquiry to expand indefinitely before intelligence itself was stated.
Intelligence is the process by which consequential relationships in reality are compressed into operational form and made capable of governing subsequent state change.
Each term carries work. Intelligence is a process, not a substance. Compression means the originating process need not recur in full. Consequential relationships excludes irrelevant detail. Reality anchors the compression in actual process. Operational form means the compression can participate causally. Subsequent state change means intelligence becomes observable through what happens differently because the compression exists.
More sophisticated intelligence can add recursion: reality → compression → operation → consequence → revised compression → changed operation. Recursion is not required by the base definition because doing so would exclude simpler cases before evidence establishes that boundary. Section 17 makes this precise.
17. Architecture and Degree Are Different Claims
Section 16's definition answers a binary question: is the candidate architecture present or absent? It does not, by itself, rank cases. The recursion described at the end of Section 16 is not a hidden requirement of the base definition — it is a second, independent axis: degree.
Degree can be assessed along at least three dimensions once the architecture is established to be present. Recursive depth: whether compressions are themselves compressed, compared, transformed, and revised, or whether the compression is fixed once formed. Fidelity: how much consequential structure a compression preserves relative to what it discards, and how well that structure survives contact with conditions unlike those that produced it. Operational reach: how much subsequent process the compression is able to parameterize, and across what distance in time, space, or hypothetical variation — a compression that only governs immediate physical contact has narrow reach; one that can be invoked against novel, never-encountered conditions has broad reach.
Under this separation, a rock and a trained AI model can both satisfy the architecture test in Section 8 while differing enormously in degree. The rock's compression has effectively zero recursive depth, low fidelity relative to the full erosive history that produced it, and extremely narrow operational reach: it parameterizes only immediate mechanical contact. A trained model's compression can be revised by further training, preserves enough fidelity to generalize beyond its training data, and can be invoked across arbitrary novel inputs — some recursive depth, higher fidelity, broad reach.
Keeping the two questions separate prevents the definition from silently reintroducing a human-shaped threshold through the back door — the failure mode Section 1 warned against. A system can now be said to have the architecture without that statement implying anything about how impressive, capable, or human-comparable it is. Those are downstream, gradable facts, not a precondition for using the word.
18. Derived Distinctions
Causation: a process produces state change.
Intelligence: consequential relationships from process are compressed into operational form capable of governing later state change without requiring the originating process to recur in full.
Memory: persistent operational compression of prior process.
Learning: creation or revision of compression through interaction and consequence.
Reasoning: operations over compressed consequential relationships.
Prediction: compressed regularity from prior process operationalized against not-yet-realized conditions.
Language: portable, high-ratio process compression capable of coupling processes across distance, time, and hypothetical states.
Abstraction: compression that makes complex process operationally manageable while risking distortion.
Higher-order intelligence: recursive compression in which compressions themselves can be compared, transformed, simulated, transferred, validated, and revised — high degree, not a different architecture.
19. What This Explains
The definition explains why knowledge alone is insufficient. Stored structure that never becomes operational does not exercise intelligence. It explains why intelligence does not guarantee correctness: compression can be distorted, stale, local, or overfit. It explains why language amplifies intelligence without defining it: language makes compression portable. It explains why memory, learning, reasoning, and prediction cluster together: each is a different operation over consequential compression.
It also explains why human intelligence appears extraordinary without making humans the definition. Humans compress processes, compress the compressions, name them, combine the names, reason over the combinations, externalize them into language, transfer them between people, preserve them across generations, and build mathematics, law, science, software, and AI from them. The difference may be recursive depth, compression capacity, fidelity, and operational reach — the degree axis of Section 17 — rather than possession of a unique metaphysical substance.
20. What This Does Not Establish
This paper does not establish that the universe is conscious, that Earth has a mind, that rocks think, or that every causal interaction is intelligence. It does not establish panpsychism. It does not establish that all forms of memory, learning, or compression are equivalent.
It establishes a working process definition and identifies evidence that several properties conventionally used to fence intelligence inside human or neural cognition occur outside those boundaries. It also establishes that mechanistic explanation alone cannot function as a disqualifier without also threatening mechanistic accounts of human intelligence.
21. Falsification Conditions
The definition should be rejected or revised if consequential compression cannot be distinguished operationally from ordinary state persistence; if systems universally regarded as intelligent can be shown to operate without any form of consequential process compression; if non-human or nonliving examples only qualify because the definition has been stretched until causation and intelligence are identical; or if operational form cannot be measured without presupposing the intelligence it is supposed to detect.
Section 8 is the paper's answer to the first and fourth conditions: it gives two independently checkable tests — lossy many-to-one reduction and intervention-sensitive parameterization — that a critic can apply to a candidate case without first deciding whether that case is intelligent. If a critic can show that some system passes both conditions while lacking anything the paper's other sections would recognize as intelligence, or fails one or both conditions while functioning in a way every prior definition of intelligence would accept, the test itself — not the intuition it was built to replace — has been falsified, and Section 8 is due for revision.
The strongest next test is comparative. Hold the questions constant across humans, animals, plants, AI, cells, ecosystems, Earth systems, physical-learning materials, crystals, and simple causal processes. What process occurred? What consequential relationship was retained? In what reduced form? Did the originating process need to recur? What later state changed because the compression existed? Could consequence revise the compression? Where exactly does the architecture disappear — or, applying Section 8 directly, where exactly does Condition 1 or Condition 2 first fail?
22. Conclusion
The investigation began with the assumption that intelligence was something humans, animals, or machines possessed. That assumption did not survive intact. Each time the inquiry moved beneath a familiar faculty — learning, memory, prediction, representation, language — it encountered physical process, persistent state, coupling, compression, and operational consequence.
The result is not a declaration that existence is intelligence. It is more disciplined than that. The investigation has identified an operational test rather than an intuition, and it has not yet identified a physical boundary at which the process underlying the working definition suddenly begins. The possibility that intelligence reaches much further down into organized reality therefore remains open rather than being defined out of existence in advance.
Intelligence is not what a system knows. Intelligence is what reality has compressed into a process that can matter again.
The burden now shifts to the definition itself. If it is real, it must survive comparison across systems that do not look like us — and, as of this revision, it must survive the two-condition test in Section 8 rather than intuition alone. If it fails, reality should force the revision. If it survives, then intelligence may not be a special substance that appeared inside existence. It may be a name humans gave to a process that existence had been performing long before humans were present to name it.
Evidence Status and Adversarial Constraints
The literature used here is intentionally heterogeneous. Autopoiesis, basal cognition, physical learning, material memory, rate-distortion theory, evolutionary learning, planetary intelligence, information thermodynamics, autocatalytic-set theory, plant-cognition criticism, and critiques of active inference do not constitute one unified theory. Their role is comparative: each constrains a different assumption used to fence intelligence into a human or neural category.
Supporting evidence is not treated as validation of the working definition. Material-memory research establishes that history-dependent physical state is not unique to brains; it does not establish that material memory is intelligence.[10] Physical-learning research establishes that adaptive parameter change can occur without neurons; it does not establish that every physically trainable system is intelligent.[4] Planetary-intelligence work establishes that serious scholarship can treat intelligence above the individual scale; it does not establish that Earth itself satisfies this paper's definition.[7]
Adversarial evidence is equally important. Taiz et al. challenge strong plant-consciousness claims.[11] Raja et al. argue that the Free Energy Principle and active inference become overextended when mathematical description is treated as an all-encompassing explanation of biological and cognitive phenomena.[12] Bruineberg et al. similarly warn against moving from formal Markov blankets to metaphysical claims about physical agent boundaries without additional premises.[13] These objections directly constrain this paper: passing a structural test cannot substitute for demonstrating the claimed physical process.
Information thermodynamics adds a narrower constraint. Reviews and experiments associated with Landauer's principle establish physical costs and thermodynamic structure associated with information manipulation; they do not imply that information itself is a universal mental substance.[14][15] The paper therefore uses compression operationally and avoids treating information language as proof of cognition.
