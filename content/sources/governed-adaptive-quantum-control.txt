DOCUMENT PURPOSE
This document provides a proposed systems architecture and operational implementation for governed
adaptive control in fault-tolerant quantum systems.
It is intended as a technical handoff to organizations possessing the quantum hardware, laboratory
infrastructure, control systems, engineering personnel, decoding expertise, and physical test environments
required to instantiate and evaluate it.
The contribution does not ask the receiving organization to accept the architecture as correct. It defines the
architecture strongly enough that the receiving organization can:
 derive a platform-specific technical specification
 determine whether the required trust boundaries are implementable
 map the design onto an existing quantum-control stack
 test the runtime behavior
 measure cost and latency
 inject faults
 verify whether the enforcement mechanisms actually work
 reject the architecture if experiment does not support it
The contribution intentionally stops before platform-specific physical engineering realization.
That boundary reflects the distinction between SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE AND
OPERATIONAL IMPLEMENTATION and PLATFORM-SPECIFIC ENGINEERING
REALIZATION.
This document defines the former. The receiving quantum engineering organization determines the latter.
TECHNICAL BASIS
The proposed architecture is grounded in known fault-tolerant quantum-computing constraints, while the
architecture itself remains an unvalidated proposal. The external evidence establishes the problem
conditions, not the success of this solution.
 DOE Quantum Genesis Q targets scientifically relevant fault-tolerant quantum systems with at least 100
logical qubits and hundreds of millions of fault-tolerant operations. DOE also states that validation must
span the full computing stack, including physical hardware, quantum gates, logical architectures,
algorithms and applications, and classical control systems. [1]
 Google Quantum AI reported below-threshold surface-code memories on superconducting hardware with
an integrated real-time decoder; the work explicitly identifies decoder throughput, long-duration stability,
leakage, and correlated error events as practical fault-tolerance constraints. [2]
 Experimental work on leakage removal shows that leakage can accumulate and spread correlated errors
through repeated quantum-error-correction cycles, and that active removal can stabilize logical
performance. [3]
 Recent calibration-conditioned decoder research demonstrates an explicit separation of timescales
between slow hardware calibration drift and low-latency syndrome decoding, supporting the relevance of
adaptive classical control around QEC. [4]
1. TECHNICAL CONTEXT
Fault-tolerant quantum computing requires more than low physical-qubit error rates.
Quantum error correction combines multiple physical qubits into protected logical qubits and suppresses
logical error when the physical system operates below the relevant error-correction threshold. Current

4. CENTRAL GOVERNING LAW
No domain may define the governing standard, judge conformity to that standard,
execute under that standard, and certify the result of that execution within the same
undifferentiated authority boundary.
Proposal generation, action admissibility, governing authorization, execution, consequence observation,
validation, evidence custody, baseline admission, policy activation, and operator authority remain separately
custodial functions.
The architecture therefore separates EVIDENCE, INTERPRETATION, PROPOSAL, AUTHORITY, EXECUTION,
CONSEQUENCE, VALIDATION, and LEARNING.

DOMAIN G - GOVERNING AUTHORITY AND POLICY CUSTODY
Domain G owns the governing objects against which operational decisions are judged. Its responsibilities
include baseline admission, authority-epoch issuance, adaptive-envelope activation, threshold-policy
activation, degraded-mode authorization, Fast Path authority classes, learned-policy promotion, revocation,
and governed operator authority.
Domain G does not generate ordinary control proposals, execute physical control, or certify its own
operational consequences.
Domain G authorizes the governing regime. Domain C evaluates a particular proposal
against that regime. Domain E independently validates the consequence.
OPERATOR / HUMAN AUTHORITY
A human operator is an authority principal acting through Domain G rather than an authority outside the
architecture. Operator scope is explicit, versioned, time-bounded, and ledgered.
Every operator is represented by an OperatorAuthorityRecord defining operator identity, role, permitted
actions, prohibited actions, device or region scope, effective time, expiration, and authority epoch. Every
consequential human intervention produces an OperatorActionReceipt.
An operator may revoke authority, request halt, request recharacterization or recalibration, and approve
supervisory transitions where policy permits. An operator may not rewrite evidence, convert DISCONFIRMED
to CONFIRMED, bypass required Reality Validation, silently replace a baseline, silently expand an adaptive
envelope, or act outside granted scope.

TRUST-BOUNDARY CONFORMANCE TEST
A conforming platform-specific implementation must demonstrate, through permission tests, isolation tests,
fault injection, or equivalent evidence, that Domain B cannot write Domain C or G authorization state;
Domain C cannot modify baseline, envelope, threshold, or epoch state; Domain D cannot generate its own
authorization or modify post-action validation records; Domain E cannot modify pre-execution expectation
records; Domain F cannot alter operational state; and Domain G cannot rewrite prior evidence.
The engineering mechanism is platform-specific. The required separation is not
optional and is falsifiable at implementation time.
A proposal generator that can directly rewrite its own admissibility result does not
conform.

11. OBSERVATION LAW
Observed data and inferred condition remain separate.
Example: OBSERVATION - A localized syndrome-event rate increased over N correction rounds.
INTERPRETATION - The increase may reflect calibration drift.
The second statement cannot overwrite the first. ObservationRecord remains authoritative regarding what
was measured. EstimationRecord remains authoritative only regarding what was inferred.

18. WHY THE ENVELOPE EXISTS
Real-time control may operate at speeds where full supervisory review is impossible. The architecture
therefore allows some authority to be established in advance.
A local real-time controller may be authorized to change a calibration parameter by a narrow amount. It may
not infer that a much larger change is permitted.
The envelope separates LOW-LATENCY PRE-AUTHORIZED CONTROL from NEW
CONTROL AUTHORITY.

function request_transition(session, target_state, supporting_records):
    current = session.current_state
    if not transition_allowed(current, target_state):
        return REJECTED_ILLEGAL_TRANSITION
    required = required_records(current, target_state)
    if not all_records_present(required, supporting_records):
        return REJECTED_MISSING_EVIDENCE
    if not ledger_chain_valid(supporting_records):
        return REJECTED_INVALID_LINEAGE
    if authority_required(target_state):
        if not authority_current(session):
            return REJECTED_STALE_AUTHORITY
    if policy_required(target_state):
        if not policy_valid(session):
            return REJECTED_INVALID_POLICY
    commit_transition_record()
    return ACCEPTED

function evaluate_admissibility(proposal):

33. EXECUTION CONTRACT
The Executor may perform only the action encoded in a valid AdmissibilityReceipt.
Immediately before actuation it verifies session_id, authority_epoch, baseline_id, envelope_id, action_type,
target, magnitude, timing validity, receipt status, and receipt expiration.
The Executor may not widen scope, substitute a different action, or change expected success criteria.
34. REAL-TIME PATH
Fault-tolerant systems may require microsecond-class or otherwise platform-limited classical processing.
Real-time surface-code experiments already demonstrate continuous streaming syndrome decoding and strict
throughput constraints. [2]
The architecture therefore distinguishes FAST PATH from SUPERVISORY PATH.
 Fast Path: syndrome classification
 real-time decoding
 logical-frame updates
 leakage-responsive actions
 local calibration corrections supported by an active FastPathQualificationRecord
 bounded local control actions
The Fast Path operates only within pre-established authority.
FAST PATH QUALIFICATION
"Previously validated" is a ledger-verifiable status, not a descriptive label. An action class may enter the Fast
Path only when a FastPathQualificationRecord exists and Domain G has issued a current
FastPathAuthorityRecord.
FastPathQualificationRecord references action class, baseline family, device scope, ValidationReceipts, test
count, failure count, validated parameter range, applicable code configuration, applicable decoder
configuration, maximum latency, recovery path, authority epoch, and expiration.
Fast Path execution requires a valid qualification record, active FastPathAuthorityRecord, current epoch,
compatible baseline, active envelope, and valid action-instance admissibility assessment.
Domain G authorizes the action class. Domain C admits the specific action instance.
Domain D executes only the admitted instance.

Learning does not become active policy by implication. The required promotion path is: LearningRecord ->

PolicyActivationReceipt.
Only a PolicyActivationReceipt changes active governance. If activation affects a baseline, adaptive envelope,

advances.
LearningRecord is not policy. PolicyCandidateRecord is not active policy. Learning
may recommend governance; it may not activate itself.
36. DECODER RELATIONSHIP
The decoder is a specialized estimator within the quantum control system. It receives syndrome information
and attempts to infer an error pattern or correction representation.
The decoder may be matching-based, clustering-based, neural, hardware-conditioned, hybrid, or another
validated design. The architecture does not prescribe one decoder. It governs how decoder output gains
operational authority.
37. DECODER OUTPUT IS NOT DIRECT AUTHORITY
For latency-critical corrections already covered by validated decoder policy, decoder behavior may execute
through the Fast Path.
For adaptive changes to decoder behavior itself, decoder output becomes an input to the governance path.
Examples include changing decoder weights, noise assumptions, leakage handling, model parameters, or
decoding strategy.
The decoder may adapt. The decoder may not silently redefine the authority under
which it operates.

function validate_consequence(execution, consequence, expectation):

72. BOUNDED CLAIM
This document does not establish that the proposed architecture improves fault-tolerant quantum computing.
That requires physical implementation and experimental evidence.
The claim is narrower: fault-tolerant quantum computing depends on a coupled quantum-classical control
stack whose assumptions, calibration, decoding, timing, and physical behavior may change during operation.
This document supplies an enforceable architecture for testing whether live characterization, bounded
adaptive authority, independent admissibility, tamper-evident evidence custody, explicit state transitions,
independent consequence validation, and governed recovery can improve the reliability of that coupled stack.
73. FINAL PRINCIPLE
The architecture does not assume the controller will always be correct. It assumes the
opposite.
The estimator can be wrong. The decoder can be wrong. The calibration can become stale. The proposal can
be wrong. The executor can fail. The validator can disagree. The telemetry can be corrupted. The system can
lose track of what it thought was true.
The architecture therefore does not attempt to establish trust by assertion. It establishes a chain in which
evidence can contradict interpretation, authority can reject capability, execution can be separated from
success, validation can reject expectation, failure can revoke authority, and historical evidence cannot be
silently rewritten to make the system appear correct.
The machine may adapt. The control system may learn. But neither is allowed to
certify reality on its own behalf.
Reality retains the last vote.
73A. TECHNICAL BASIS - FULL-STACK VALIDATION
DOE Quantum Genesis Q identifies a target of at least 100 logical qubits and hundreds of millions of fault-
tolerant operations. DOE also describes validation and verification spanning physical hardware, quantum
gates, logical architectures, algorithms and applications, and classical control systems. This supports the
system-level premise of the handoff: classical control is inside the fault-tolerance boundary rather than
outside it. [1]
73B. TECHNICAL BASIS - REAL-TIME DECODING
Below-threshold surface-code experiments have integrated real-time decoding into repeated error-correction
operation. The cited Nature work reports a 1.1 microsecond QEC cycle and approximately 63 microseconds
average real-time decoder latency for the reported distance-5 implementation, while also discussing scaling
pressure on decoding throughput. [2] This motivates the split between low-latency pre-authorized Fast Path
operation and slower supervisory authority.
73C. TECHNICAL BASIS - LEAKAGE AND CORRELATED ERRORS
Experimental work has shown that leakage can persist and spread correlated errors across repeated QEC
cycles, and that active leakage-removal mechanisms can improve stability. [3] Below-threshold work also
reports rare correlated-error events and residual unmodeled error contributions. [2] These results motivate
live characterization, explicit model applicability, and the rule that the current decoder or calibration model
remains separate from current physical evidence.

73D. TECHNICAL BASIS - ADAPTIVE DECODING
Calibration-conditioned decoding research exploits the difference between slower hardware-calibration drift
and low-latency decoding. [4] The architectural relevance is the same even when the decoder implementation
changes: a changing physical environment may justify adaptive decoding, but the authority to alter the
decoding regime remains distinct from decoder inference itself.
73E. TECHNICAL BASIS - BOUNDED INTERPRETATION
The cited external literature establishes real constraints - real-time decoding, drift, leakage, correlated errors,
and full-stack validation. It does not establish that Governed Adaptive Quantum Control is correct. This
document therefore retains an experimental posture: the architecture must be instantiated, compared against
relevant baselines, and rejected or revised if its added authority separation, evidence custody, or adaptation
logic fails to produce measurable net benefit.
