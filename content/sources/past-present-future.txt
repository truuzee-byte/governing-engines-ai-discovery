PAST. PRESENT. FUTURE.
How Humanity Industrialized Representation - and What We Could Point Outward
Next
DeMarius J. Lawson
Governing Engines LLC
Research Edition v1.2 | September 2026
We spent centuries making it cheaper to represent the world. The next
opportunity may be making it cheaper to keep asking the world
whether our representations are right.
Research scope
A comparative systems analysis of ten historical technologies, ten technological forms introduced from
2022-2025, a present-day reality-facing control set, and the demonstrated components that could support
machine-extended physical inquiry.
Evidence posture
This paper distinguishes documented purpose, demonstrated capability, observed deployment direction,
architectural inference, and open possibility. It does not treat model output, corporate positioning, or
internal metrics as independent evidence of external outcomes.
Abstract
Technological history is often narrated as a sequence of inventions. This paper examines a different
object: direction. It asks what constraint a technology initially addressed, what capability it introduced,
what later optimization concentrated on, and what ultimately settles whether the technology worked.
Across ten historical cases, the evidence does not support a simple story in which technologies begin
"real" and later become corrupted by abstraction. Instead, it reveals two intertwined lineages. Some
technologies primarily expand access to external reality; others expand humanity's ability to reproduce,
transmit, compute, distribute, generate, and operate representations. General representational
substrates - especially computers, networks, and the Web - permit human-created abstractions to
accumulate inside them because they can process a ballistic trajectory, a tax code, a financial
instrument, a game economy, or a scientific measurement with the same underlying machinery.
The present-day sample shows a further transition. Several important technologies introduced since
2022 are born several layers deep inside this accumulated symbolic environment: conversational AI,
generative image editing, autonomous software engineering, computer-use agents, and browser agents.
Counterexamples are equally important. AlphaFold 3, patient-specific gene editing, fusion ignition, the
James Webb Space Telescope, asteroid sample return, Mars oxygen production, and autonomous
laboratories show that technological civilization remains strongly engaged with external reality.
The resulting claim is therefore not that humanity has abandoned reality. It is that representation
throughput has undergone repeated industrial revolutions while evidence acquisition remains

Artificial intelligence and robotics create an opportunity to narrow this asymmetry. Machine reasoning
can generate and manage representations at extraordinary speed; robotics, autonomous laboratories,
distributed instrumentation, and manufacturing can extend evidence acquisition into environments,
durations, and experimental volumes that human embodiment cannot easily sustain. But embodiment
alone does not give reality authority over a reasoning model. Reality determines physical occurrence;
evidence custody preserves what was observed; and a governed, challengeable process external to the
model must control claim-state transitions when evidence conflicts with interpretation.
The future opportunity is not necessarily a mythical intelligence "smarter than humanity." It may be
persistent infrastructure that lets humanity investigate beyond ordinary human physical reach while
keeping evidence separate from interpretation, preserving contradiction, and denying any single model,
person, committee, or instrument unilateral authority to certify a claim. The paper concludes that the
next frontier may be not merely machines that produce more representations, but systems that make it
cheaper to keep asking reality whether those representations are right.
1. Research Question
Are we using our newest capabilities primarily to discover how far our
abstractions can go - or are we equally willing to use them to discover
how far reality goes?
This question began with a narrower disagreement about artificial intelligence. Contemporary AI
systems can retrieve, transform, and generate information at speeds no individual human can match.
That performance is frequently described as greater intelligence. Yet much of the information available
to these systems consists of human-produced representations: language, laws, financial systems,
software, academic categories, organizational rules, cultural artifacts, scientific models, and records of
prior observation. More information about representations is not automatically more direct knowledge
of reality.
The distinction matters because a civilization can become extraordinarily capable inside its own
symbolic environment while leaving large portions of external reality inaccessible to sustained inquiry.
The question is not whether abstraction is bad. Abstraction is one of civilization's greatest technologies.
Language, mathematics, maps, scientific models, software, institutions, and money all allow humans to
coordinate and reason beyond immediate perception.
The question is whether the abstraction remains connected to the reality from which it derives meaning
- and whether new technological capacity is used only to deepen the abstraction or also to expand the
evidence available from outside it.
2. Method and Analytical Instrument
The paper uses comparative case tracing rather than a universal quantitative index. Ten historical
technologies were selected across communication, observation, mechanical power, computation,
networking, navigation, and publishing. Ten present technological forms were purposively selected
under a strict date rule: the specific product or capability form had to be introduced between 2022 and

Each case is evaluated through eight questions: initial constraint; initial purpose; capability introduced;

The method deliberately avoids several shortcuts. Commercialization is not treated as equivalent to

claims are evidence of stated positioning, not proof of capability. A model prediction is not treated as
physical evidence. A technology can be highly abstract and still remain externally settled. Conversely, a
physical robot can operate in reality while optimizing an entirely human-created metric.
3. PAST - What We Built, and Where It Went
The historical sample does not support a morality tale in which inventions begin pure and later become
corrupted. It supports a systems story. Technologies that remain dependent on physical settlement tend
to preserve strong coupling to external reality. Technologies that operate on general representations
become available to whatever symbolic systems civilization chooses to place inside them.
3.1 Printing Press
The printing press attacked the physical and labor cost of reproducing written representation. Movable
type did not determine whether the reproduced symbols were true, false, scientific, religious,
commercial, legal, or fictional. Its achievement was representation throughput: text could be reproduced
at a scale unavailable to manuscript copying. The press therefore belongs primarily to the
representation-expansion lineage. Its epistemic neutrality is important: increasing the velocity of
claims does not itself increase the truth of claims.
3.2 Telescope
The telescope extended human observation beyond unaided vision. Galileo's early astronomical use
made previously inaccessible celestial evidence available to direct observation. Subsequent optimization
- larger apertures, improved optics, photography, spectroscopy, non-visible wavelengths, orbital
observatories, Hubble, and Webb - repeatedly returned to the same external target: acquire more
evidence from the universe. The telescope is a strong counterexample to any claim that technological
optimization naturally drifts inward.
3.3 Steam Engine
Newcomen's atmospheric engine addressed mine drainage and mechanical work. Later improvements
increased efficiency and generalized steam power across manufacturing and transportation.
Commercial pressure did not detach the technology from reality; it often rewarded better physical
performance. Steam therefore demonstrates that economic optimization can intensify reality-facing
capability when physical work is itself the valuable outcome.
3.4 Telegraph
The telegraph reduced communication latency across distance. It was representational from birth:
electrical signals carried messages. Railroads, news, commerce, finance, government, and personal

3.5 Telephone
The telephone extended vocal communication beyond biological hearing range. Like the telegraph, it
was representation-expanding from the start. Its later absorption into smartphones and general
computing should not be mistaken for a linear evolution of the telephone alone. The important pattern
is capability absorption: communication became one function inside a more general representational
machine.
3.6 Radio
Radio extended communication without physical wires. Broadcasting then introduced an economic
problem: continuous transmission needed a funding model. Advertising helped convert audience
attention into an optimization target. The electromagnetic capability remained physical and unchanged,
while the objective layered on top of it shifted. Radio shows how optimization pressure can change
without changing the underlying physical technology.
3.7 Electronic Computer
ENIAC was developed primarily for high-speed calculation of artillery firing tables, but programmable
computation was general. Once something could be formalized symbolically, the computer could
operate on it. Scientific equations, payroll, tax rules, financial instruments, games, simulations,
advertisements, and software all became computational material.
This representational generality is central to the later accumulation of abstraction. A computer can
calculate a planetary trajectory and an advertising auction without caring which represents external
physics and which represents a human-created market.
3.8 ARPANET and the Internet
A major early motivation for ARPANET and the Internet was resource sharing among expensive
geographically separated computers. Email quickly became a dominant application, and the network
evolved into general infrastructure for communication, information, commerce, entertainment, and
institutional operations. The Internet Society explicitly describes general-purpose infrastructure as a
defining feature. The network did not abandon its purpose so much as become a substrate for purposes
that could not have been exhaustively specified at launch.
3.9 GPS
GPS provides positioning, navigation, and timing. It now supports logistics, surveying,
telecommunications, finance, and many other systems, including highly abstract ones. Yet its core
service remains externally anchored to physical position and time. GPS therefore demonstrates that a
representational system can be deeply embedded in abstraction while retaining strong external
settlement.
3.10 World Wide Web
The Web was conceived at CERN to meet demand for automated information sharing among scientists
and institutions. Its open architecture then supported publishing, commerce, search, social platforms,
entertainment, institutional interfaces, and software applications. The Web was representational from

3.11 What the Past Actually Shows
The historical evidence supports four broad classes: reality-extension technologies, representation-
extension technologies, general representation substrates, and mixed systems. The most
consequential transition occurs when a technology becomes sufficiently general that other human
abstractions can be built inside it.
By the beginning of the twenty-first century, humanity had spent
centuries making its representations increasingly reproducible,
transmissible, programmable, networked, and accessible. When
generative AI arrived, it did not encounter an empty world. It
encountered a civilization already translated into symbols.
That historical buildup matters. AI did not independently choose to become abstraction-heavy. It arrived
inside an enormous machine-readable environment built by prior technologies.
4. PRESENT - Machines Born Inside the Abstraction Environment
The present sample is intentionally narrow. It does not represent all important technologies introduced
in the last five years, and no percentage claim is inferred from it. Its purpose is to examine a striking
contemporary condition: important new technologies can now be born several layers deep inside
systems humanity previously constructed.
4.1 ChatGPT - 2022
OpenAI released ChatGPT in November 2022 as a conversational research preview designed to answer
follow-up questions, admit mistakes, challenge incorrect premises, and gather feedback about strengths
and weaknesses. Its later development expanded into coding, research, files, search, software
interaction, and organizational workflows.
The key point is not that it was launched for business. It is that a general conversational machine
immediately found an enormous environment of existing representations to operate.
4.2 Apple Vision Pro / visionOS - 2023
Apple introduced Vision Pro as its first spatial computer, explicitly describing it as blending digital
content with the physical world. Yet its launch proposition prominently included apps, productivity,
entertainment, communication, and virtual displays.
Spatial computing therefore demonstrates that moving an abstraction into physical space does not
necessarily change the underlying activity. It also supports reality-coupled applications, making it a
mixed case rather than a simple inward one.
4.3 Photoshop Generative Fill - 2023
Adobe introduced Generative Fill to add, extend, or remove image content through text prompts and
explicitly positioned it as a way to accelerate creative ideation, exploration, and production.

4.5 Devin - 2024

This is a technology born directly inside accumulated abstraction: computers produced software

4.6 Sora - 2024
OpenAI connected Sora to research toward general-purpose simulators of the physical world. Yet the
demonstrated output is generated video. This creates an important epistemic category: reality-modeling
through synthetic representation.
A generated physical-looking event is not physical evidence that the event occurred. A simulation can
become more realistic without becoming an observation. For a claimed world simulator, external
settlement would require more: the system would need to make discriminating predictions about
physical outcomes and those predictions would need to survive independent observation or experiment
outside the generator. Visual plausibility alone cannot establish physical understanding.
4.7 AlphaFold 3 - 2024
AlphaFold 3 materially expanded biomolecular prediction across proteins, nucleic acids, small
molecules, ions, and modified residues. It is representation-heavy but reality-targeted: the molecular
systems it predicts exist independently of the model.
Its predictions are not Reality Primacy. Experimental biology remains external. AlphaFold 3 therefore
demonstrates abstraction in service of reality inquiry rather than abstraction as the destination.
4.8 Claude Computer Use - 2024
Anthropic introduced computer use by noting that a vast amount of modern work happens through
computers. The model can interpret screens and issue mouse and keyboard actions.
The rationale itself captures the historical condition: humans moved work into computers, then built AI
capable of operating the computers. The machine increasingly participates inside the abstraction
environment rather than merely carrying representations through it.
4.9 Operator - 2025

4.11 What Present Shows - and Does Not Show
This sample does not establish that most modern technology is inward-facing. It does establish that
several important technological forms can now be born deep inside accumulated abstraction.
Past representational technologies largely copied, moved, or computed representation. Present AI
increasingly interprets, generates, navigates, and acts through representation. That is a real
transition.
5. CONTROL GROUP - Reality Did Not Stop
A thesis this broad becomes useless if it ignores counterevidence. The 2021-2026 period also contains
major reality-facing advances.
Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory achieved laboratory fusion ignition in December 2022 after
decades of work. The James Webb Space Telescope expanded observation of the early universe, star and
planet formation, and distant worlds. OSIRIS-REx returned physical material from asteroid Bennu to
Earth in 2023; later analysis identified amino acids and all five nucleobases used in terrestrial genetics
while explicitly not establishing evidence of life. NASA's MOXIE produced oxygen from the Martian
atmosphere sixteen times. The FDA approved the first CRISPR/Cas9-based therapy in 2023. Autonomous
laboratories such as A-Lab connected computational planning to hundreds of physical materials
experiments.
These cases kill the claim that technological civilization has abandoned reality.
They expose a better mechanism.
Reality-facing inquiry remains vigorous, but physical inquiry carries physical friction.
A million additional language-model interactions can be produced primarily by allocating more
compute. Another asteroid sample requires spacecraft, launch, navigation, years of transit, sample
acquisition, return, recovery, and laboratory analysis. Fusion requires extreme physical conditions and
specialized facilities. Space telescopes require large physical systems that must survive deployment and
operation far from Earth.
Reality did not become cheap because computation did.

6. The Asymmetry: Representation Throughput vs. Evidence
Throughput
Humanity has progressively removed different constraints from representation.
The printing press reduced the cost of reproduction. Telecommunications reduced the cost of distance
and latency. Computers reduced the cost of calculation and transformation. Networks and the Web
reduced the cost of distribution and access. Search and cloud systems reduced the cost of storage,
retrieval, and computational availability. Generative AI reduces the cost of production and
manipulation.
Representations can now be created, copied, transformed, transmitted, stored, searched, recombined,
and regenerated at extraordinary scale.
Physical evidence cannot scale in the same way. A representation of Mars can travel essentially at light
speed. A physical instrument still has to get to Mars. A model can generate ten thousand candidate
materials computationally; physical synthesis still requires matter, energy, equipment, and time. A
model can generate a thousand experimental hypotheses; reality does not owe it a thousand
experiments per second.
This produces a fundamental asymmetry:
Representation can increasingly scale independently of the physical
processes it represents. Evidence cannot.
The danger is therefore not abstraction itself. It is asymmetrical scaling. A civilization can become
more productive informationally without becoming proportionally more knowledgeable empirically.
6.1 Claim Throughput and Verification Throughput
The printing press increased access to claims. Radio and television increased their reach. The Internet
increased distribution. Social platforms reduced publication friction. Generative AI increases not merely
distribution but production.
Yet none of these technologies automatically increases the truth of the resulting claim.
The modern condition can therefore be described as a growing mismatch between Claim Throughput
and Verification Throughput. More AI checking AI may increase internal evaluation capacity, but
unless external evidence enters the loop, the process can remain representational recursion.
6.2 Why Capability Falls Inward
Abstraction has structural advantages as a destination for new technology. It is digitally accessible.
Feedback is fast. Economic value is often easier to represent. Existing infrastructure can be reused.
Failure is frequently cheaper. Institutions also operate under finite funding, career, product, and
political horizons.
This can create an optimization gradient toward problems where value can be measured quickly and
infrastructure already exists. The gradient is not universal: public research institutions, universities,
national laboratories, observatories, and basic-science programs were built in part to support inquiry
whose applications and economic returns may be uncertain or distant.

A company can calculate the return from reduced labor hours or improved conversion rates. The return
from investigating an unknown geological phenomenon for thirty years may be impossible to price in
advance. U.S. research funding patterns illustrate the distinction: business finances most total R&D,
while federal support remains especially important to basic research, where economic returns are
typically longer-horizon and more uncertain.
This paper uses Discovery Legibility in a deliberately narrow sense: the degree to which the prospective
value of investigating an unknown can be represented inside the decision system allocating resources
before the discovery occurs.
When a resource-allocation system requires prospective value to be specified before inquiry begins,
poorly legible unknowns face a structural disadvantage. Basic-research institutions are a deliberate
countermechanism to that disadvantage, not evidence that it does not exist.
7. External Settlement and Contradiction Authority
The analysis requires another distinction. Physical interaction alone is not enough.
A warehouse robot can physically pick up boxes while optimizing an entirely abstract metric such as
units per hour. A digital twin can be highly representational yet remain useful only if it corresponds to a
physical machine. AlphaFold can produce sophisticated molecular predictions while experimental
biology retains the ability to contradict them.
The critical question is not simply whether a technology is physical or abstract. It is where the claim
settles.
7.1 Operational Settlement
Operational settlement asks whether the intended operation occurred.
Did the robot move the box? Did the code compile? Did the transaction complete? Did the spacecraft
execute the maneuver?
7.2 Epistemic Settlement
Epistemic settlement asks whether a representation corresponds sufficiently to something
independently existing.
Did the predicted structure match physical measurement? Did the material survive the claimed load?
Did the map correspond to terrain? Did the scientific hypothesis survive experiment?
A system can have excellent operational settlement and poor epistemic settlement.
7.3 Contradiction Authority and Claim-State Governance
Even external evidence is insufficient if the reasoning system retains authority to explain away every
contradiction without changing the status of its claim. But moving that authority to a human, committee,
instrument, or second model does not solve the problem by itself. The relevant authority must belong to
a governed process rather than to an actor.
The key architectural requirement is therefore:
When externally preserved evidence conflicts with a model's
representation, the model must not possess sole authority to preserve
the claim as established. A claim-state process external to the model

should apply explicit evidence requirements and transition rules to
determine what the available evidence currently permits the system
to claim.
The model may propose that a sensor malfunctioned. That is a legitimate new hypothesis. It may request
replication. It may identify contamination or calibration error. But it cannot transform an observed
failure into an event that did not occur. The external process is itself fallible, so its decisions require
provenance, preserved contradictions, independent challenge, revalidation when new evidence arrives,
and rollback when a prior promotion is no longer supported.
Occurrence is not interpretation.
Worked example - bounded claim demotion
Claim: Material X remains structurally intact at 1,000 MPa under test conditions Y. Current claim state:
SUPPORTED.
New evidence: three independently preserved, calibrated tests record failure at 620-640 MPa under the
same bounded conditions Y. The reasoning model proposes that the instrumentation may be defective.
Governed transition: SUPPORTED -> PENDING_REVALIDATION. The transition occurs because the
promoted claim now conflicts with evidence satisfying the predeclared test conditions. The model may
request calibration checks, replication, or an alternative causal test, but it may not retain SUPPORTED
solely by explaining away the contradiction.
If independent replication confirms failure, the bounded original claim transitions
PENDING_REVALIDATION -> DISCONFIRMED. If an instrumentation defect is independently established,
the conflicting evidence is marked invalid with provenance preserved and the claim is re-evaluated; it is
not silently restored. The example does not make the process infallible. It shows where authority sits:
transition rules govern claim status, while reality remains outside the process and determines what
actually occurred.
For a physical vessel, reality itself supplies consequence. The ground supports the vessel or it does not.
The shielding survives or it does not. The material breaks or it does not. The vessel cannot override
physical consequence.
A reasoning model has no equivalent automatic relationship. Physical consequence reaches it only as
information. Therefore evidence custody and claim-state governance must remain external to the model
if reality is to retain authority across the boundary from physical consequence to symbolic reasoning.
Reality determines what occurred; the governed process determines only what the available evidence
currently justifies claiming.
8. FUTURE - Industrializing Inquiry
The future opportunity is not to eliminate abstraction. It is to close the loop between abstraction and
reality more often, across more environments, at greater scale, and over longer periods than human
embodiment currently allows.
The relevant question is not whether AI can become a mythical mind infinitely smarter than humanity.
A system can create extraordinary value simply by combining capabilities humans already struggle to
sustain simultaneously: high information throughput, large memory, continuous operation, rapid

retrieval, distributed sensing, physical instrumentation, robotic execution, and long-duration
experimental continuity.
8.1 Four Frontiers of Machine-Extended Inquiry
Spatial frontier. Machines can operate in environments humans cannot practically inhabit: deep ocean,
subsurface environments, extreme radiation zones, other planetary bodies, and eventually more distant
destinations.
Temporal frontier. Machines can observe processes continuously across periods longer than individual
careers and potentially longer than individual human lifetimes.
Scale frontier. Instruments can operate at microscopic, molecular, astronomical, or massively
distributed scales beyond ordinary human perception and attention.
Experimental-volume frontier. Autonomous laboratories and distributed instruments can execute and
monitor more physical experiments than individual human researchers can personally perform.
These are not substitutes for human science. They are extensions of where human inquiry can reach.
8.2 Demonstrated Components Already Exist
The complete architecture described here has not been demonstrated. Its individual components,
however, increasingly have.
NASA's Autonomous Sciencecraft Experiment demonstrated onboard event detection, scientific
prioritization, planning, and observation changes on the EO-1 spacecraft. NASA's Starling mission has
demonstrated distributed spacecraft autonomy in which multiple spacecraft coordinate observations
and workloads with limited ground intervention. A-Lab performed 353 materials experiments during 17
days of closed-loop operation, connecting computation, robotics, machine learning, and active learning
to physical synthesis. NASA has used computational generative design to produce mission hardware
optimized against engineering constraints. The Deep Space Network already supports communication
with interplanetary spacecraft.
MOXIE crossed another important boundary on Mars: local extraterrestrial material was processed into
a useful product. Across sixteen runs, the experiment extracted oxygen from the Martian atmosphere.
NASA's broader in-situ resource utilization program explicitly investigates using local water, oxygen,
metals, regolith, and atmospheric gases to reduce dependence on Earth and enable longer-duration
exploration.
These demonstrations do not establish a self-sustaining autonomous off-world research factory. They
establish that many of the required primitives are no longer hypothetical.
8.3 Evidence Horizons and Architectural Extrapolation
To keep the Future section from converting component demonstrations into system-level proof, this
paper uses three evidence horizons.
Horizon I - Demonstrated components: bounded capabilities already shown in operation, such as
autonomous scientific decision-making, robotic experimentation, deep-space communication, generative
engineering, long-duration machine operation, and limited extraterrestrial resource processing.
Horizon II - Active engineering and foreseeable integration: capabilities under development or plausible
combinations of demonstrated components, including greater ISRU, robotic assembly, more autonomous
manufacturing, distributed sensing, and higher-capacity space communications.

Horizon III - Architectural extrapolation and open possibility: the unproven integration of these
capabilities into persistent adaptive research/manufacturing infrastructure, including dynamically
translating new evidence requirements into newly designed and manufactured instruments over long
durations.
A future research system could combine those trajectories into a persistent loop:
human question -> reasoning -> required evidence -> required physical interaction -> instrument or
vessel specification -> manufacture/deployment -> physical consequence -> evidence custody -> governed
external claim-state decision -> revised question.
The reasoning system need not physically inhabit the research vessel. Keeping primary reasoning
infrastructure on Earth can be advantageous: compute can be maintained and upgraded, humans
remain connected to objectives and consequential decisions, and the physical research layer can remain
replaceable.
A communications network can carry bounded research packages outward and evidence inward. Local
systems require operational autonomy because light-speed delay prevents Earth from remaining inside
every low-level control loop. Navigation, collision avoidance, thermal management, fault recovery, safe
shutdown, and communications recovery can therefore be local without granting the remote system
independent epistemic authority over its scientific claims.
The off-world physical layer can be treated as instrumentation rather than as the intelligence itself.
8.4 Replaceable Embodiment
The unanswered question should determine the embodiment.
A machine investigating a planetary atmosphere need not resemble a machine entering a fissure. A
distributed sensor field need not resemble a rover. A sacrificial probe need not be designed to return. A
stationary instrument intended to observe for thirty years may need no locomotion at all.
This reverses the common framing. The question is not, "What can this robot investigate?" It is:
What embodiment does this question require?
If the vessel is destroyed, the research program need not be destroyed with it. The evidence acquired
before failure can survive elsewhere. A replacement vessel can incorporate what was learned. Failure
becomes an experimental loss rather than necessarily the loss of the inquiry itself.
Do not make the machine immortal. Make the research survive its
machines.
8.5 Local Manufacturing as an Inquiry Multiplier
Space programs already investigate in-situ resource utilization because transporting every needed
kilogram from Earth is expensive. NASA explicitly frames ISRU as using resources available at mission
destinations to produce water, oxygen, propellant, building materials, and other useful products.
The deeper research opportunity is not merely to reproduce Earth manufacturing elsewhere. It is to
determine the local manufacturing envelope: what materials are actually accessible, what processing
works under local conditions, what properties locally manufactured materials exhibit, what component
classes can be made reliably, and which inputs still require Earth.
That creates an outward-facing compounding loop:

investigating it.
8.6 A Factory as a Physical Question Compiler - Architectural Extrapolation
One possible long-horizon architecture is a research/manufacturing system capable of translating an
unresolved scientific question into physical requirements when existing instruments cannot obtain the
necessary evidence. This capability has not been demonstrated end to end.
The proposed translation is: unknown -> evidence requirement -> interaction requirement -> instrument
requirement -> manufacturable design -> physical artifact.
If such integration became reliable, the resulting artifact could enter the environment and convert
previously inaccessible physical conditions into evidence. The important claim is architectural, not
predictive: several bounded precursors exist, but their composition into a persistent autonomous design-
manufacture-deploy-investigate loop remains unresolved engineering.
Current obstacles include interoperability among AI, hardware, and software; robotic maintenance and
repair; reproducibility and trust in self-driving laboratories; autonomous manufacturing from local
feedstocks; cooperative robotic interfaces; and sustained operation without human servicing.
Generative engineering, autonomous science, robotic manufacturing, distributed sensing, and ISRU are
therefore precursors rather than proof of the complete architecture. Under the evidence horizons
defined in §8.3, the 'physical question compiler' belongs to Horizon III: an architectural extrapolation
whose feasibility depends on solving those integration gaps.
8.7 Longitudinal Machine Inquiry
Human research institutions can outlive individuals, but experimental continuity remains vulnerable to
funding changes, staff turnover, fragmented records, equipment replacement, disciplinary boundaries,
and shifting priorities.
A machine-extended research architecture could preserve a tighter lineage across decades: what was
observed; how it was observed; raw evidence where practical; instrument state and environmental
conditions; processing applied; interpretation at the time; uncertainty; later challenges; and claim-state
history.
That distinction matters because future understanding may make old evidence newly important. An
anomaly dismissed in Year 7 may become meaningful after a discovery in Year 79. If only the conclusion
"sensor artifact" survives, the future inherits the past's interpretation. If the evidence and provenance
survive, the future can interrogate what actually happened.
The future should inherit the past's evidence, not merely the present's
conclusions.
8.8 One Hundred Years Without Predicting Year One Hundred
A century-scale machine research program should not be justified by pretending to know what it will
discover. That would reproduce the epistemic error the architecture is designed to resist.

The honest sequence is a progression of questions. Early years may establish what is present, what can
be accessed, what can be processed, and what can be manufactured reliably. Later years may use that
capability to reach environments inaccessible to the original instruments. Those environments may
expose phenomena that require new measurements. New evidence may demand new embodiments.
If the program succeeds, its later questions should not all be predictable at launch.
A successful century of inquiry should produce questions its designers
were incapable of specifying at the beginning.
9. Why This Is Not a Superintelligence Argument
The term "superintelligence" often combines several different advantages: information volume, retrieval
speed, processing speed, memory, reasoning quality, and autonomy. Those dimensions should be
separated.
A machine may retain more recorded information than any individual human could encounter in a
lifetime. It may retrieve that information faster. It may compare more observations simultaneously.
Those advantages can produce performance beyond individual humans without proving that the
machine possesses a fundamentally superior form of intelligence.
The more useful question for this paper is what happens when machine throughput is connected to
evidence humans could not otherwise acquire.
A system with one hundred years of preserved experiments, millions of observations, thousands of
failed hypotheses, and continuous environmental measurements occupies a radically different
informational position from a human researcher. Its advantage may be best described not as mystical
intelligence but as superhuman experimental continuity.
That continuity matters only if the evidence remains distinguishable from the system's interpretations of
it.
10. Implications for Technological Direction
This paper does not argue that civilization should stop optimizing software, commerce, communication,
administration, entertainment, or other human-created systems. Those systems are real parts of human
life and often produce physical consequences.
The argument is about option space.
When access to powerful general-purpose technology is allocated through systems that reward fast
feedback, measurable returns, existing infrastructure, and low failure cost, those problems can attract
capacity quickly. That can produce enormous value. It can also leave possibilities underexplored when
their value is difficult to specify before discovery.
This is why basic science, public research institutions, observatories, national laboratories, and space
agencies matter. They are institutional countermechanisms for investigating questions whose value
cannot always be represented in advance.
AI creates a new version of that allocation question.

Do we use machine capability only to operate the symbolic world we already built more efficiently? Or
do we deliberately invest some of that capability in expanding the empirical boundary of what
humanity can observe, test, and understand?
The choice is not binary. AI can process invoices and investigate Europa. Robots can move warehouse
inventory and enter environments humans cannot survive. Generative models can create
advertisements and propose hypotheses for externally controlled experiments.
The issue is whether outward inquiry receives deliberate infrastructure rather than being expected to
emerge automatically from the same incentives that reward short-term abstraction optimization.
11. Limitations and Counterclaims
Several limitations constrain this paper's conclusions.
First, the case sets are purposive analytical samples, not statistically representative inventories of
technological development. The present sample intentionally includes culturally visible technologies and
cannot support claims about the prevalence, percentage, or economy-wide share of modern innovation
directed inward or outward. Its narrower use is comparative: it demonstrates that important new
technologies can now originate several layers inside accumulated representational infrastructure.
Second, the categories are not mutually exclusive. A technology can expand reality access while
operating through representations. GPS, digital twins, AlphaFold, autonomous vehicles, and scientific
simulations demonstrate this. The key distinction is not whether abstraction exists, but whether external
evidence remains capable of constraining claims.
Third, economic incentives are not inherently hostile to reality-facing inquiry. Commercial pressure
improved steam engines, supports launch systems, finances biotechnology, and can accelerate scientific
instrumentation. The argument is narrower: short feedback loops and legible returns can create a
structural advantage for abstraction-facing applications.
Fourth, physical evidence is not automatically interpreted correctly, and an external claim-state process
is not infallible merely because it sits outside the model. Instruments can fail; data can be contaminated;
experiments can be poorly designed; validation rules can be wrong; and human reviewers can be
biased. Evidence authority therefore requires calibration, provenance, replication, independent
methods, preserved contradiction, challenge paths, revalidation, explicit uncertainty, and rollback
rather than naive trust in any single measurement or decision-maker.
Fifth, the Future architecture remains an extrapolation. Autonomous science, robotic experimentation,
generative design, deep-space communications, ISRU, and long-duration machine operation exist in
bounded forms. Their integration into persistent adaptive research/manufacturing infrastructure has
not been demonstrated, and current self-driving laboratories and robotic systems still face substantial
interoperability, maintenance, reproducibility, trust, and human-servicing requirements.
Finally, no architecture removes reality's physical constraints. Distance, energy, radiation,
thermodynamics, communication latency, material limits, and experiment duration remain. Machines
can reduce some human-imposed costs of inquiry. They cannot negotiate the conditions of reality itself.
12. Conclusion
Humanity has not stopped exploring reality. It has done something more complicated.

[Source PDF page 16]
GOVERNING ENGINES  |  PAST. PRESENT. FUTURE.
DeMarius J. Lawson  •  Governing Engines  •  September 2026   |   16
Over centuries, we built technologies that made representation increasingly reproducible, transmissible,
programmable, networked, accessible, and now generative. Those technologies created enormous
symbolic environments in which modern civilization operates. Contemporary AI arrived inside that
environment and immediately became useful there.
That is not a failure. It is the predictable result of the world we built.
But it is not the boundary of what machine capability can become.
The next technological frontier may not require a machine that "knows" more human abstractions than
any person. It may require infrastructure that lets machines help humanity acquire more evidence than
human bodies, human lifetimes, and human physical reach currently permit.
Robotics can extend where computation touches reality. Autonomous laboratories can increase
experimental volume. Distributed instruments can increase observational coverage. Local
manufacturing can shorten physical iteration cycles. Replaceable vessels can make some failures
affordable. Long-duration evidence custody can allow future understanding to revisit past observations.
None of those capabilities removes the need for human purpose, scientific judgment, or external
epistemic authority.
In fact, greater machine capability makes those separations more important.
The reasoning model should not become custodian of the evidence against itself. The vessel does not
need a philosophy of Reality Primacy; reality already supplies consequence. The model does. Evidence
must cross from physical consequence into symbolic reasoning without becoming subordinate to the
representation it may contradict, and claim-state transitions must remain governed by an external,
challengeable process rather than by any single actor.
Human abstraction gave civilization extraordinary reach. The task is not to abandon it. The task is to
remember what it is for.
Use abstraction as an instrument for encountering reality - not as a
substitute for it.
We already know how to send AI through our paperwork, our software, our markets, our images, and
our institutions.
The remaining question is where else we are willing to point it.
We spent centuries industrializing representation. The next frontier
may be industrializing inquiry.

Appendix B. Evidence Boundary for the Future Architecture
Demonstrated in bounded form: autonomous scientific decision-making; distributed spacecraft
autonomy; robotic physical experimentation; computational generative engineering; deep-space
communications; extraterrestrial resource processing; long-duration robotic science; local-resource
research and simulated-regolith manufacturing/construction work.
Active engineering / foreseeable integration: greater ISRU capability; more autonomous
manufacturing; distributed sensing; increasingly capable robotic assembly; higher-capacity space
communications; more adaptable mission hardware; stronger autonomous fault management.
Architectural extrapolation: persistent Earth-based reasoning connected through communications to off-
world manufacturing nodes that could, if major integration problems are solved, translate new evidence
requirements into newly designed and manufactured specialized vessels while preserving independent
evidence lineage and externally governed claim-state transitions.
Open possibility: what decades or a century of such inquiry would discover, which new materials or
phenomena would matter, what questions would emerge, and how far the resulting infrastructure could
extend human empirical reach.
Not claimed: AGI; superintelligence; fully self-replicating factories; unlimited autonomy; elimination of
communication delay; elimination of physical risk; or a demonstrated end-to-end system matching the
proposed architecture.
